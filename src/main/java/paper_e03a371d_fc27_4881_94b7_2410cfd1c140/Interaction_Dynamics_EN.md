## Abstract

Two black boxes. Mutually impenetrable (P2 holds in both directions), mutually unintelligible (P3 is lossy in both directions), each unaware of what it wants (P2 seals it off from itself), Semantic Flow running in its own direction on each side. Under these conditions, any transaction is structurally impossible.

Yet humans transact billions of times every day.

Economics, game theory, and transaction cost theory all treat the transaction as the default state — rational agents find a point of mutual exchange and transact. What needs explaining is failure. This paper inverts that: the transaction is what needs explaining.

Starting from the postulates of Semantic Flow Dynamics and adding one condition — that some signal sources in the signal field also have Semantic Flow — the Coupled Loop and its structural consequences are derived. On this foundation, the paper answers: given that P2 and P3 both hold, how does a transaction ever occur? The answer is Functional Convergence — not understanding, not optimization, but two opaque Collapse sequences that happen to land on a point both parties can accept.

The natural landing point of the derivation: the supply-and-demand curve is not a description of a mechanism but a statistical description of the Collapse residue of large numbers of black boxes under physical constraints. The validity of economics does not require the rational-agent assumption — only constraint conditions and large numbers. Bubbles, failures of the law of one price, brand premium, panic selling — none of these are anomalies requiring patches; they are natural implications of the framework.

This paper adds no new postulates. P1–P4 are sufficient.

**Keywords:** Interaction Dynamics, Coupled Loop, Functional Convergence, Transaction, Statistical Residue, Risk Compression

[Derived] In Semantic Flow Dynamics, your Collapse output is already unpredictable (Law of the Black Box). The effect of the other party's response upon reaching you is unpredictable once more (Law of the Black Box returning to your side). [Derived] Each of your Collapses is irreversible. After every Collapse, the loop is no longer the loop it was before that Collapse. ← Law of Flux + irreversibility of Collapse

There is no equilibrium state. Game theory presupposes the existence of an equilibrium — two rational players will converge to a stable strategy combination. The Coupled Loop does not have this property. The loop's structure has already changed after each round; "settling down" is not possible.

## XVI. Relationship to Existing Theories

**Classical economics (Smith, Marshall).** Supply meets demand; the price mechanism adjusts; the invisible hand. Transaction is the default state. The framework inverts this: the transaction is what requires explanation. The supply-and-demand curve is not a description of a mechanism but a statistical description of the Collapse residue of large numbers of black boxes under constraints. Economics thinks it is describing a mechanism; it is actually describing Statistical Residue. The validity of economics does not require the rational-agent assumption — only constraint conditions and large numbers.

**Game theory (von Neumann, Nash).** Options known, payoffs known, rational choice presupposed. None of these hold in wet market reality — options are not exhaustible (Law of the Black Box); payoffs are not quantifiable (Law of Dissipation + Law of the Black Box); choice is not the output of computation (Law of the Black Box). Game theory becomes an effective approximation when Institution has compressed Semantic Flow to the point of negligibility. The framework defines game theory's boundaries of applicability.

**Behavioral economics (Kahneman, Tversky).** Acknowledges that actual behavior deviates from the rational model. But the framework is the same: first presuppose a rational baseline, then list deviations. The framework's position is different: no rational baseline exists. The Law of the Black Box says behavior cannot be determined by input — not that it deviates from some determinable baseline, but that the baseline itself does not exist.

**Transaction cost economics (Coase, Williamson).** Asks why some transactions go through firms rather than markets. The premise is that transaction itself is unproblematic; the question is which organizational form to use. The framework says the premise itself needs examination: transaction itself is not unproblematic — it is something that happened to work under constraint conditions.

**Information economics (Akerlof, Stiglitz).** Information asymmetry causes market failure. The framework says: it is not that information is "asymmetric" — asymmetry presupposes a symmetric possibility exists. The Law of the Black Box says opacity is structural, not repairable. Give both parties exactly the same information; Semantic Flows run in their own directions; outcomes are still unpredictable.

**Bourdieu's habitus.** Closest to the framework. Behavior is not calculated but bodily-disposed; agents do not know what they are doing, yet behavior has pattern. Convergence point: behavior is the product of sediment. Divergence: habitus is a static description and does not track how it is altered in real time during Interaction. The framework's Coupled Loop tracks this alteration.

**Geertz's bazaar economy.** Core observation: the fundamental problem of the bazaar is not price but information search. Highly consistent with the framework — Bilateral Opacity plus non-standardization equals extreme uncertainty. Geertz is anthropological description without formalization.
