**Terminological Note**

This comparison observes each religion's attitude toward knowledge itself — through changes and distinctions at the level of lived knowledge — rather than the contents each religion arrives at regarding knowledge. Post-mortem Reckoning: the final settlement at death, applicable to individuals, groups, and civilizations. All proper nouns (Spirit Realm, Realm of the Unseen, dependent origination) are labels, not theological definitions. Borrowing nouns from each tradition to describe the epistemic limit does not reflect actual scriptural meaning.

**Zhi (知):** In this paper, Zhi is information — the signal entering consciousness. It is not knowledge (knowledge), not cognition (cognition), not understanding (understanding). "It will rain tomorrow" is a Zhi, regardless of whether you understand it, believe it, or find it significant — those are products of what happens after Zhi enters Xin. Zhi is the raw material; Xin determines what the raw material becomes. The Zhi of this paper is taken from the concept of Signal in information theory — a Signal can be copied, transmitted, and stored, independent of the receiver's state. Shannon's information theory ends here. This framework picks up from this point: the Signal enters consciousness, meets Xin, and produces Semantic Flow. Shannon governs how Signals are transmitted; this framework governs what happens to Signals after they arrive.

**Xin (信):** In this paper, Xin is not faith (faith), not belief (belief), not trust (trust). Xin is the functional state of consciousness — a mixture of values, preferences, and will; one could also say it is the totality of human conscious functioning: reason combined with humanity. This is present as long as one is alive, and absent only in death. Even after his enlightenment, the Buddha retained preferences (choosing to teach rather than remain silent), will (forty-five years of propagating the Dharma), and judgment (using different methods for practitioners of different capacities). Buddhism's "letting go" is releasing attachment at the semantic end — specific semantics produced after Zhi has been processed by Xin — not shutting down Xin itself. A living consciousness cannot be without Xin.

"Xin precedes Zhi" does not mean "faith precedes knowledge." It means: before receiving any information, consciousness already has a pre-existing state for processing that information (values and will). This state determines what semantics the same piece of information is processed into. This is a description of the precondition of information processing, not an epistemological proposition.

# **Introduction**

All three major religions acknowledge the existence of a domain that human reason cannot reach on its own, and each has constructed an entire system around this acknowledgment. This is not the peculiar claim of any single religion — it is a gate set at the same position by three independently developed civilizational systems. The existence of this gate is an observable fact: Christianity, Islam, and Buddhism all presuppose a Cognitive Limit; the difference lies in their strategies for handling it. This paper does not argue whether this Cognitive Limit is epistemologically valid. It only records that all three major religions behave as though it is, then analyzes their respective paths of information flow on each side of the gate.

Religion is the meaning-generating system with the longest duration and the widest reach in human history. The three major religions differ in their strategies for handling the Cognitive Limit: Christianity acknowledges that election and reprobation have no reason; Islam acknowledges that guidance and misguidance have no reason; Buddhism acknowledges that in the domain beyond conceptual discourse, there is unknowing. Each has developed different strategies for handling this boundary, and on this basis has constructed entire civilizational structures. Understanding how these strategies operate is an important task for understanding human civilization.

Religion is not a belief system — it is a flow system of information and meaning. This redefinition is not a rhetorical strategy but an analytical necessity. When we discuss religion as a belief system, discussion invariably slides into doctrinal comparison, doctrinal comparison invariably slides into contests of position, and contests of position have no exit. For thousands of years, theologians, philosophers, and atheists have walked this road countless times without ever reaching consensus. This paper chooses a different route.

If religion is an information flow system, then the tools for analyzing it should not be theology but the dynamics of information flow. The tool this paper uses is Semantic Flow Dynamics (v5.0, UUID: 40a22ff8-9d90-4e1e-82f6-1fd80917c139) — a framework describing what happens when information flows between consciousnesses. The framework starts from two primitives — Individual, Stimulus — and three definitions — Xin, Semantic Flow, Signal — and builds four postulates. The core postulate, the Law of the Black Box, states: the next-moment direction of Semantic Flow cannot be determined by its current direction together with the incoming Signal. From the postulates are derived three basic functions — Filtering, Transformation, Collapse — along with their properties; from the basic functions is derived the Positive Feedback Loop.

The developmental order of this paper and the framework must be stated here. Three Religions × Five Dimensions came first — an observation of structural differences among the three major religions at the cognitive level. This observation raised a question that existing theories could not answer: why can an extremely small number of core religious claims be amplified into entire civilizational structures, and why did the three major religions walk completely different paths? Semantic Flow Dynamics was built to answer this question. This paper is the result of using the framework to revisit the original observation. This means the religious cases are the generative cases for Semantic Flow Dynamics, not its verification cases — just as Newton induced universal gravitation from an apple; you cannot use the apple case to verify universal gravitation. Verification of the framework must come from independent cases — phenomena not used in building the framework (see Chapter 4).

Under this postulate system, religion presents an exceptionally unusual information-theoretic phenomenon: the input end is extremely small, the output end is extremely large. Christianity's core claims can be compressed to the twelve sentences of the Apostles' Creed. Islam's core claims can be compressed to the single sentence of the Shahada. Buddhism's core claims can even be compressed to a single word: suffering (dukkha). Yet these minimal inputs, flowing over thousands of years, produced legal systems, ethical norms, artistic traditions, political institutions, wars and peace — entire civilizational structures.

This disproportionate information gain is the core question this paper seeks to explain. How does amplification occur? What different dynamic paths did the three major religions walk? Why have these religions persisted for thousands of years while other systems claiming supernatural knowledge have long since perished?

The structure of this paper is as follows. The prefatory Three Religions × Five Dimensions is a compressed comparison table presenting cross-sections of epistemic attitudes across five cognitive levels in the three major religions on a single page. Chapter 1 establishes the Five-Stage Model: the cognitive topology from Xin to Death. Chapter 2 defines Trans-Limit Knowledge and locates it within the model — without arguing about its source, only recording Source Claims. Chapter 3 analyzes three dynamic paths — Christianity's Gu-King Path, Islam's Locking Path, and Buddhism's Self-Organizing Path — and within the path comparison unfolds the information flow comparison across the three religions. Chapter 4 discusses the framework's limitations and challengeable conditions.

## **Methodological Statement**

The analytical layer of this paper is information behavior analysis, not theological comparison. This distinction must be stated at the outset because it determines what this paper can and cannot do. Even more important to clarify is the relationship between this paper and the framework: the observation of Three Religions × Five Dimensions preceded the construction of Semantic Flow Dynamics; the framework was built to explain this observation. Therefore this paper is a generative case for the framework — it demonstrates the framework's explanatory power, but cannot serve as verification. Verification must come from independent phenomena not considered when the framework was built.

The analytical layer of this paper is information behavior, not the truth or falsity of doctrines. The judgments this paper makes are structural (for example: Channel closure makes interpretation the sole direction of evolution), not theological.

The methodological position of Semantic Flow Dynamics is: the framework does not judge the truth or falsehood of semantic content (Semantic Flow Dynamics, Section IX, 9.5 Truth Boundary); it does not judge good or bad, right or wrong (Section IX, 9.6 Value Boundary). This paper inherits this position.

This paper engages in dialogue with the cognitive science of religion. Pascal Boyer (2001) and Justin Barrett (2004) explain from cognitive science why humans readily accept supernatural ideas — the brain has a tendency toward over-attribution of agency. This paper does not dispute this explanation, but neither does it build upon it. This paper's analytical layer is not "why people believe" but "how knowledge flows after they believe." These are questions at different levels.

# **Chapter 1: The Five-Stage Model — The Cognitive Topology**

Any conscious existence can be mapped onto a fixed topological structure. This structure is not the product of religion — it is the product of cognition itself. Religion is one of the systems operating on this structure. This chapter establishes this structure.

## **1.1 The Starting Point: Xin**

The framework's starting point is not birth — it is Xin.

Xin consists of values and will. Values are your pre-existing tendencies for judging good and bad, right and wrong; will is your innate drive. The operational logic of all three major religions presupposes that Xin Precedes Zhi — they all first require an adjustment of attitude before transmitting knowledge. This is not coincidence; it is structural necessity: without Xin, Trans-Limit Knowledge cannot enter; the Bible becomes merely a historical document, the Quran merely an Arabic text, the Buddhist sutras merely philosophical discourse. "Xin Precedes Zhi" is not an epistemological proposition proposed by this paper — it is the shared operational premise of all three major religions. This paper observes this premise without arguing whether it is epistemologically correct. After contact with information, values may collapse and reorganize — that is the process of Transformation, which does not change the temporal priority.

Xin Determines Semantics. The same Zhi, entering different Xin, produces completely different semantics. The same sentence "it will rain tomorrow," entering a farmer's Xin versus entering an office worker's Xin, produces semantics that are worlds apart. It is not merely that the reaction differs — the meaning differs, the significance differs, the associated chain of thought differs, the behavior it drives differs. Zhi is the material; Xin determines everything about the material.

The first principle of each of the three major religions is an attitude, not knowledge. Christianity begins with fear of Yahweh. Islam begins with fear of Allah. Buddhism begins with *wu* — the denial of a First Cause is itself an attitudinal position. None of the three begin by giving you a body of information and letting you judge it; all begin by requiring you to adjust the state of Xin, and only then begin transmitting knowledge. This is not coincidence — it is structural necessity: without Xin as the activating condition, knowledge is Noise; the Signal arrives, is processed, but nothing grows from it. "Religion does not stand without Xin" is not rhetoric — it is a dynamics fact of information flow.

## **1.2 Within the Cognitive Limit: The Main Battlefield of Civilization**

Once Xin activates, consciousness begins to receive, process, and produce knowledge. This process takes place within the Cognitive Limit — the domain of knowledge that humans can experience, reason about, and verify.

Within the Cognitive Limit is the main battlefield of civilizational construction. Agriculture, architecture, law, medicine, science, art — all observable, verifiable, and cumulative achievements of knowledge exist in this domain. This domain does not require religion to function; science and philosophy do very well here.

But within the Cognitive Limit there is one special phenomenon with religious relevance: a small quantity of Trans-Limit Knowledge is amplified into entire civilizational structures. This amplification process is the subject of Chapter 3. Here it is only necessary to mark the position: within the Cognitive Limit is not only the territory of natural knowledge — it is also the soil in which supernatural knowledge takes root and grows.

## **1.3 The Cognitive Limit: The Gate**

The Cognitive Limit is the central node of the Five-Stage Model. It is the boundary at which humans acknowledge "I do not know" — not because information is insufficient (that can be addressed by gathering more), but because the nature of the question exceeds the capacity of human cognitive tools.

What happens after death? Why does the universe exist rather than not exist? What is the nature of consciousness? What is the ultimate ground of morality? All three major religions place these questions in the domain that human reason cannot resolve on its own — whether or not this classification holds epistemologically, it is the shared operational premise of the three religions, and it is where the gate is set.

The Cognitive Limit is not a wall — it is a door. And the key to opening this door is Xin. Without Xin, the Cognitive Limit is a wall: one stands on this side, acknowledges "I do not know," and stops. This is the path philosophy takes; Socrates' "I know that I know nothing" is a calm acceptance of the Cognitive Limit. Science takes a different path: it attempts to advance the Cognitive Limit, but not to cross it — science only handles falsifiable propositions; questions beyond the Limit fall outside its jurisdiction.

Religion takes a third path: it directly claims to have transported knowledge from the other side of the door. Revelation, awakening, divine decree — all of these are claims that "Trans-Limit Knowledge has arrived within the Cognitive Limit." And the believer's Xin determines whether they accept this transport. If Xin does not open, the gate does not open, and knowledge cannot enter.

## **1.4 Beyond the Cognitive Limit: The Claimed Source of Religious Knowledge**

Beyond the Cognitive Limit is the domain of unverifiable knowledge. "Unverifiable" does not mean "nonexistent," nor does it mean "existent" — it means: humanity's current cognitive tools cannot make a determination about the contents of this domain.

The three major religions describe this domain in completely different ways. Christianity calls it the Spirit Realm, claiming it is real, active, and conscious, capable of revelation and deception alike. Islam calls it the Realm of the Unseen (al-ghayb), claiming it is the foundation of all created order, capable of guidance and misguidance alike. Buddhism handles this domain in a fundamentally different way — it does not name this domain, does not describe it, and even questions whether this domain needs to be discussed at all. "In the domain beyond conceptual discourse: unknowing" is Buddhism's most honest attitude toward what lies beyond the Cognitive Limit.

This paper uses the neutral term "Beyond the Cognitive Limit" and does not use any religion's proper nouns. Spirit Realm, Realm of the Unseen, beyond conceptual discourse — these are labels borrowed by each tradition, not analytical categories of this paper. This paper needs only one fact: all three major religions acknowledge the existence of a domain that human reason cannot reach, with divergence concerning the nature of this domain and its relationship to humanity.

## **1.5 Death: The Antithesis of Xin**

Death is the terminal of information flow. In the terms of Semantic Flow Dynamics: consciousness disappears; Xin and Semantic Flow simultaneously return to zero — no process, no stages (Law of Death, P4). What can persist is only Signal Containers — books, institutions, architecture, images. But a Signal is not semantics; reading a book left by a dead person, the semantics produced belong to the reader, not to the dead. The dead person's semantics are no longer anywhere.

Death is not only a terminal — it is the antithesis of Xin. Xin activates all semantics; Death terminates all semantics. This antithesis constitutes the tension of the entire system: if Death is the absolute end, then all semantics activated by Xin ultimately return to zero — what is the significance? This question was not invented by philosophers; it is the structural predicament that every consciousness will eventually confront.

Post-mortem Reckoning. Death is the settlement point, but the one who settles is not the dead — it is the living. You die; others render judgment on your life. A religion dies; history renders judgment on that religion. A civilization dies; the civilizations that come after render judgment on that civilization. You have no say from inside the coffin. Post-mortem Reckoning is the analytical perspective of this paper — it is not a negation of religious eschatology. This paper does not judge whether reincarnation or eternal life is real; it only marks an observable fact: from the outside, Death is the settlement point. A Buddhist may believe there is another life, but from the perspective of Post-mortem Reckoning, this life's settlement is already complete in others' eyes. What you believe does not affect how others judge you. Post-mortem Reckoning applies equally to individuals, groups, religions, and civilizations — once the coffin lid closes, it is closed.

Death is the hardest event beyond the Cognitive Limit. No scientific method can tell you what happens after death — not "not yet possible," but "impossible in principle," because Death is unrepeatable, unobservable, and unverifiable. It presses back from the terminal on the entire system, compelling it to provide an answer. Xin activates the system; Death compels the system to be complete. The tension between these two ends is the dynamic reason for religion's existence.

## **1.6 The Structural Basis of the Five Stages**

The Five-Stage Model is not an arbitrary division — it is a structural fact of cognition. Three of its elements are physical conditions: within the Cognitive Limit is the domain of verifiable knowledge; the Cognitive Limit is the boundary of human reason; Death is the physical termination of consciousness. These three do not depend on any religious doctrine; remove all religion and they remain valid. The starting point (Xin) is a position adopted by the framework — this framework holds that any consciousness already has values and will before receiving information (see the discussion in 1.1); this position is contestable, but the framework starts from it. The only "soft" element is beyond the Cognitive Limit — its existence is a fact (human reason has a boundary; beyond that boundary there is logically a domain), but what is in that domain is a claim.

These three lines (Xin, Cognitive Limit, Death) cut cognitive space into exactly two domains (within the Cognitive Limit, beyond the Cognitive Limit). Remove Xin, and the system has no activating condition; remove Death, and the system has no terminal; remove the Cognitive Limit, and the verifiable and unverifiable merge, and the distinction between religion and science disappears. No more parsimonious division has been found — any additional dividing line can be subsumed into the internal structure of within the Cognitive Limit or beyond the Cognitive Limit, with no need to add a new position at the topological level. If anyone can propose an alternative topology requiring fewer or more nodes, with explanatory power equal to or greater than this model, the structure of this paper needs revision.

# **Chapter 2: The Definition and Location of Trans-Limit Knowledge**

This chapter does not argue the source of Trans-Limit Knowledge. The source question falls outside this paper's jurisdiction — just as Semantic Flow Dynamics does not explain where Xin comes from (Xin is a primitive, irreducible), this paper does not explain where Trans-Limit Knowledge comes from. What this chapter does is more limited and more solid: defining what Trans-Limit Knowledge is, locating it within the Five-Stage Model, and recording each religion's Source Claim.

## **2.1 Definition**

The operational definition of Trans-Limit Knowledge: knowledge that a religious system claims to originate from the other side of the Cognitive Limit.

This definition has only one criterion: the claim. This paper does not judge whether the claim is true — is "there will be judgment after death" truly revelation from God, or human construction? This paper does not address this question. This paper only records one fact: Christianity claims this knowledge comes from God; Islam claims it comes from Allah; Buddhism claims it comes from the realization of an Awakened One. The claim itself is an observable fact; no judgment of the truth or falsity of the claim is required.

This definition inherits the Channel definition of Semantic Flow Dynamics: a Channel carries a self-declared Signal but does not carry a verifiable source. This paper records the existence and behavior of Source Claims without adjudicating whether Source Claims are correct.

## **2.2 Why Not Argue the Source**

Readers may expect this paper to argue that Trans-Limit Knowledge "truly" comes from the supernatural, or that it is "merely" human construction. This paper does neither — not out of evasion, but out of methodological consistency.

Xin is the primitive of Semantic Flow Dynamics — the framework starts from it without being responsible for explaining it. The source of Xin is a black box (Xin is irreducible, inexhaustible, observable only through its effects). The source of Trans-Limit Knowledge occupies the same epistemological position in this paper: it is the premise of the analysis, not its conclusion. The three major religions each claim to possess Trans-Limit Knowledge; after this Source Claim enters within the Cognitive Limit, it produces observable effects — amplification into civilizational structure. What this paper analyzes is the effect, not the source.

This mode of treatment has one important consequence: it allows this paper to be read simultaneously by believers and non-believers. Believers may hold that Trans-Limit Knowledge truly comes from God; non-believers may hold that it is human construction — neither affects the analysis in this paper, because what this paper analyzes is the flow path of knowledge after it has entered within the Cognitive Limit, not its starting point beyond the Cognitive Limit. What the source is does not change how the amplification mechanism operates, just as where a seed came from does not change the process by which it grows into a forest.

## **2.3 The Source Claims of the Three Religions**

The three major religions' Source Claims for Trans-Limit Knowledge are entirely different, and these differences directly shaped the subsequent paths of information flow. The claims are recorded here without judgment.

Christianity claims: Trans-Limit Knowledge comes from divine revelation. God is an active, conscious, willful existence who chooses to reveal to humanity. The Channel of revelation consists of prophets and apostles; the record of revelation is the Bible. But Christianity simultaneously acknowledges a disturbing fact: beyond the Cognitive Limit there is not only revelation but also deception. The Spirit Realm is active, capable of revelation and deception alike, and human reason cannot distinguish between the two. This means the believer's Xin is not founded on rational judgment — reason cannot help here — but on Xin itself. Election and reprobation have no reason; revelation may yet increase.

Islam claims: Trans-Limit Knowledge comes from divine revelation (wahy) from Allah. Similarly to Christianity, the Realm of the Unseen is active and conscious, capable of guidance and misguidance alike. But there is one key difference from Christianity: Islam directly denies any increase in revelation. Muhammad is the Seal of the Prophets; the Quran is the final revelation; the Channel is permanently closed. This means the stock of Trans-Limit Knowledge is fixed — no new input will arrive. Guidance and misguidance have no reason; human reason (Aql) likewise cannot distinguish between them.

Buddhism's claim is fundamentally different. Buddhism does not claim an external source actively transmitting knowledge. What Buddhism claims is: an Awakened One, through cultivation, crossed the Cognitive Limit and obtained direct cognition on the other side. The Buddha is not a Channel — he is not an intermediary "transporting knowledge from beyond the Cognitive Limit on your behalf" — but a pioneer: "the first person to arrive there, telling you how to get there yourself." Going further, Buddhism is not even certain what is "there" beyond the Cognitive Limit — in the domain beyond conceptual discourse: unknowing. There is a distinction between named discourse and what lies beyond it; this distinction cannot be bridged by speculation. Buddhism's attitude toward what lies beyond the Cognitive Limit is closest to honest unknowing.

## **2.4 The Dynamic Effects of Source Claims**

Although this paper does not adjudicate the truth or falsity of Source Claims, Source Claims themselves, as Signals entering within the Cognitive Limit, produce different dynamic effects. These effects are observable and fall within this paper's analytical scope.

Christianity's "revelation may yet increase" means the Channel is an Open Channel — new Trans-Limit Knowledge may arrive at any time. This creates a dynamic Signal environment: believers must continuously discern what is new revelation and what is deception. The Church, as an institutionalized mechanism of discernment, therefore becomes necessary.

Islam's "no further increase in revelation" means the Channel is already a Closed Channel — the stock of Trans-Limit Knowledge is fixed in the Quran and the Hadith. This creates a closed Signal environment: the answers to all questions are already in the existing texts; what is needed is interpretation, not new input. This explains the high development of Islamic jurisprudence (fiqh) — when input is fixed, the refinement of interpretation becomes the only direction of the system's evolution.

Buddhism's "individually attainable" means the Channel is not designated but is open to all practitioners. This creates a decentralized Signal environment: every awakened one is a potential Channel, with no central institution monopolizing the transmission of Trans-Limit Knowledge. This explains the plurality of Buddhist traditions — when the Channel is not monopolized, different practitioners bring back different "reports" from their respective practices; plurality is a structural necessity.

The differences among these three Source Claims — Open Channel, Closed Channel, Decentralized Channel — directly determined the dynamic paths of the three major religions. The next chapter unfolds the analysis of these three paths.

# **Chapter 3: Dynamic Paths — Three Different Ways of Generating Civilization**

This chapter is the core of the paper. What it must explain is a problem of information gain: the input end is extremely small (a few core claims); the output end is extremely large (entire civilizational structures). From the twelve sentences of the Apostles' Creed to the entire social order of medieval Europe; from the single sentence of the Shahada to Islamic civilization spanning three continents; from the Four Noble Truths to the Buddhist cultural sphere covering East and Southeast Asia — how does amplification occur?

The three major religions share the basic elements of amplification — Signal Transformation, Functional Convergence, Signal Container Institutionalization, Mortality Pressure — but the combination of these elements is completely different, producing three completely distinct dynamic paths. This is not one universal template applied three times — it is three civilizational generation processes, each with its own logic.

## **3.1 Shared Amplification Elements**

Before unfolding the three paths, the amplification elements shared by all religions are marked.

Signal Transformation. After Trans-Limit Knowledge enters within the Cognitive Limit, it undergoes Transformation in the believer's Semantic Flow, producing new semantics. The four characters "God loves the world" entering a believer's Xin, through Transformation, can produce an entire set of ethical semantics about the nature of love, the meaning of sacrifice, and the conditions of forgiveness. The single word "suffering" entering a practitioner's Xin can unfold into the complete system of the Four Noble Truths and the Noble Eightfold Path. The product of Transformation is new — neither a copy of Zhi nor a projection of Xin (Transformation: the process by which a Signal is processed within Semantic Flow; its result cannot be determined by the input). The same core claim unfolds into completely different results in different cultures — because the Signal is the same, Xin differs, and the products of Transformation vary accordingly.

Functional Convergence. Individual semantics converge functionally at the group level. Functional Convergence is not semantic convergence — the Law of the Black Box guarantees that every individual's Semantic Flow differs — but a statistical approximation of output Signal patterns. Large numbers of consciousnesses have each Transformed the core claim; at the output end, Signal patterns happen to align — similar vocabulary, similar behavioral norms, similar emotional responses. Viewed from outside, this is "the Church," "the Ummah," "the Sangha." Functional Convergence pushes the individual's Transformation products from the private domain into the public domain, forming the Positive Feedback Loop.

Signal Container Institutionalization. The products of Functional Convergence are encoded into Signal Containers — scriptures, rituals, ecclesiastical hierarchies, legal systems, architecture. Signal Containers do not think; they do not generate Semantic Flow; but they delay Drift and bridge across generations. Drift is the phenomenon of the Positive Feedback Loop's Functional Convergence point gradually deviating from the founding Signal across Generational Replacement — the observable result of P1+P2+P3+P4 operating together at the civilizational scale. Scriptures can persist for thousands of years; institutions for hundreds. Each generation of new consciousnesses receives these Signal Containers and re-Transforms them within their own Semantic Flow; the stability of the core Signal ensures that output patterns statistically approximate those of the previous generation. The continuation of civilization is not the transmission of semantics but the statistical reproduction of output patterns.

Mortality Pressure. Death, as a persistent source of pressure, continuously draws consciousness back to the system in search of meaning. Every consciousness eventually faces Death; each confrontation reactivates the Positive Feedback Loop. Mortality Pressure is the driving force shared by all religions; it is also a key difference between religion and most secular ideologies — secular ideologies' responses to Death are typically lower in intensity than religion's, and their Positive Feedback Loops depend more on the delivery of present-world conditions. Once real-world Signals diverge from claims, the loop easily breaks.

The four elements above are necessary conditions. But the key is not the elements themselves — it is the combination. The three major religions used the same elements to walk completely different paths.

## **3.2 The Christian Path: Gu-King¹**

The dynamic path of Christianity is: Transformation → Functional Convergence → formation of Positive Feedback Loop → Drift → schism → collision → mutual destruction → survivors evolve → further schism → further destruction → Gu-King emerges.

The dynamic characteristic of this Gu-King Path is: Open Channel plus no Signal locking equals continuous generation of variation. Christianity claims that revelation may yet increase (Three Religions × Five Dimensions, dimension IV); the Channel is an Open Channel; new Trans-Limit Knowledge may arrive at any moment. This means there is continuous new input at the Signal end; believers must discern what is new revelation and what is deception; the Church, as an institutionalized discernment mechanism, therefore becomes necessary. But the standard of discernment is not itself locked — different standards of discernment produce different churches; different churches produce different doctrinal interpretations; schism is a structural necessity.

After schism comes collision. Every major schism in Christian history — the East-West Schism, the Reformation, the Counter-Reformation — was accompanied by fierce competition and even violence. Collision produces selection pressure: branches with weaker adaptability wither or perish; branches with stronger adaptability survive and evolve. Catholicism, Eastern Orthodoxy, and Protestantism are the survivors of several rounds of elimination. Each survivor is more refined, better organized, and more capable of withstanding the next round of challenges than before the schism.

This is not amplification — it is evolution. Amplification means only an increase in size; evolution means structural variation plus selection pressure plus increased adaptability. The dynamics of Christianity are not as simple as "core claims amplified into civilization" — the core claims enter the Positive Feedback Loop, are continuously reinterpreted, reorganized, and adapted through repeated schisms and collisions, and the versions that survive remain recognizable at the Signal end but have become unrecognizable at the institutional and practical ends.

The prediction of the Gu-King Path: Christianity will continue to schism; after schism, branches will compete; the survivors of competition will be stronger. This prediction can be tested against historical data — if Christianity's tendency toward schism ceases, or if branches after schism do not compete, the descriptive power of the Gu-King Path is undermined.

## **3.3 The Islamic Path: Locking and Stable State**

The dynamic path of Islam is: Transformation → Functional Convergence → formation of Positive Feedback Loop → Signal locking → variation suppressed → further locking → stable community.

The dynamic characteristic of this Locking Path is: Closed Channel plus extreme Signal locking equals suppressed variation. Islam directly denies any increase in revelation (Three Religions × Five Dimensions, dimension IV); Muhammad is the Seal of the Prophets; the Quran is the final revelation; the Channel is permanently closed. The stock of Trans-Limit Knowledge is fixed — no new input will arrive.

After the Channel closes, the only remaining direction of evolution is interpretation. The principle of the Quran's untranslatability requires believers to encounter the Arabic original directly, locking the Signal at its most primordial form. The isnad (chain of transmission) system traces the complete transmission Channel of each hadith, recording every intermediate node — in the terms of Semantic Flow Dynamics, this is a complete record of the Channel. It is one of the most systematic Signal source-tracing systems in human history. When input is fixed, the refinement of interpretation becomes the sole direction of the system's evolution — this explains the high development of Islamic jurisprudence (fiqh).

The locking strategy is not without cost. The Signal end is highly unified, but schism occurs nonetheless — the Sunni-Shi'a split does not lie at the Signal end (both read the same Quran) but in the contest over interpretive authority (different judgments about succession and authority). Interpretation is behavior at the Signal end — argumentation expressed in language — but the driving force of argumentation comes from differences in Xin. But unlike Christianity's Gu-King Path, Islamic schism does not produce sustained evolutionary pressure, because both sides of the schism acknowledge the same locked Signal end. What is contested is who has the right to interpret — not whether the Signal itself should change.

The prediction of the Locking Path: Islam's Signal end will maintain high stability; variation will appear primarily at the interpretive and political ends, not in any fundamental rewriting of the Signal end. This prediction can be tested against historical data and contemporary observation.

## **3.4 The Buddhist Path: Self-Organization and Natural Succession**

The dynamic path of Buddhism is: Transformation → individual cultivation → natural succession.

Buddhism faces the same cognitive map of the Five-Stage Model, but its operational layer is fundamentally different. Christianity and Islam operate at the Signal end — Descending Mode, written into scriptures, locked or filtered. Buddhism operates at the semantic end — it points toward the contents of the black box: unspeakable, untransmittable, unverifiable. The Buddha is not a Channel — he is a pioneer: "the first person to arrive there, telling you how to get there yourself." The Buddhist sutras are not knowledge — they are Signals pointing toward semantics; the finger pointing at the moon is not the moon.

The dynamic characteristic of this Self-Organizing Path is: Decentralized Channel, no Signal locking, only market competition, no zero-sum game. Theravāda, Mahāyāna, and Vajrayāna did not emerge through schism — they each grew independently. "Growing independently" does not mean there is no competition — the number of monastic communities a given place can sustain is finite; material support is scarce; the tradition of debate among schools (as at Nalanda) was an institutionalized competition at the Signal end. But the competition here has only a market mechanism: competing for followers, for material support, for influence. Schools with weaker adaptability contract; those with stronger adaptability expand.

Buddhist competitive structure has one fundamental difference from the Abrahamic religions: no heresy. Christianity's Gu-King Path has two layers of competition — market mechanism plus zero-sum game. The market mechanism is competition for believers; the zero-sum game is the adjudication of heresy: your doctrine is wrong and you must be eliminated. The Inquisition, heresy trials, and excommunication are all institutionalized forms of the zero-sum game. Islam similarly has these two layers; the difference is that the criterion of the zero-sum game is interpretive authority rather than doctrine. Buddhism has only the first layer. The principle of skillful means (upāya) cancels the possibility of zero-sum competition at the root — you walk your path, I walk mine; both are fingers pointing at the moon; different fingers are not grounds for life-or-death conflict.

Why does Buddhism not produce an institution of heresy? Because awakening is an individual event, not institutional certification. Whether you are awakened, only you know; no institution has the standing to stamp your certificate. The heresy adjudication of Christianity and Islam can operate because there is a universally recognized certifying institution — the Church, the Ummah. The institution's authority comes from the Channel: we are connected to what lies beyond the Cognitive Limit, so we have the standing to judge whether you are right or wrong. Buddhism lacks this structure. Individual schools occasionally display exclusive behavior (Nichiren Buddhism declaring other schools to be perverse teachings; the Gelug school suppressing the Jonang school), but such behavior cannot be institutionalized into a sustained mechanism of heresy adjudication — because the criteria of the adjudicator are not recognized by other schools. Without a certifying institution, there is no institution of heresy.

Buddhism's internal patterns of information flow are not singular. Observing from the direction of Signal flow, at least three different modes exist. The first is the Ascending Mode, represented by Zen and Theravāda samatha-vipassanā: the individual through cultivation reaches the Cognitive Limit on their own, with no external input and no designated Channel — the purest form of self-organization. The second is the Quasi-Descending Mode, represented by the Pure Land tradition: Amitabha Buddha's forty-eight vows constitute an external promise; believers rely on faith, vow, and practice to reach the Pure Land; the operational structure approximates the Descending Mode; the Signal end even has locking characteristics — recitation of the Buddha's name is the repeated reception of the same Signal. But the key difference is: this "promise" does not claim to come from the active transmission of what lies beyond the Cognitive Limit, but rather provides a path option within the framework. The third is the Channel-Designated Mode, represented by Tibetan Buddhism: the tulku (reincarnate lama) lineage, initiation transmission, and the teacher as a necessary condition of cultivation — the Channel is institutionally designated. But what is designated differs from Christianity's ecclesiastical hierarchy — what is designated is "who has the standing to guide your cultivation," not "who has the authority to transmit revelation."

These three modes have coexisted within Buddhism for over a thousand years without any one eliminating the other two. This itself is a structural product of pure market competition (no zero-sum game) — without heresy adjudication to compel unification, without a certifying institution to eliminate deviators, multi-modal coexistence is the stable state, not a transitional one. This coexistence would not arise within the Abrahamic religions, because the zero-sum game layer would squeeze out modes that deviate from the main path. The diversity within Buddhism is not an expression of the framework's weak descriptive precision — it is a structural feature of the object itself. An X-ray of a crab looks fuzzier than an X-ray of a human, but that is because the crab has no internal skeleton, not because the X-ray is defective.

The costs of the Self-Organizing Path must also be recorded. Pure market competition, no doctrinal baseline, no heresy adjudication — these features that produce plurality and resilience simultaneously produce structural fragility. First, organizational dispersal: the absence of central authority means monastic corruption cannot be systematically corrected; the relaxation of precepts has no institutional response mechanism. Second, absence of resistance capacity: the extinction of Buddhism on Indian soil was not only an external event of "Signal Containers being destroyed." Facing the military invasion of a highly organized Locking-Path religion like Islam, the Self-Organizing Path lacked even the institutional capacity to mobilize resistance. This is an endogenous defect of the path, not merely a historical misfortune.

There is a seemingly contradictory phenomenon here: Buddhism claims the beyond is beyond conceptual discourse, yet the volume of the Buddhist canon is greater than that of Christianity and Islam combined. The root of the contradiction is precisely the structural product of operating at the semantic end. Semantics cannot be spoken, but to convey "semantics cannot be spoken," you must use Signals — use language to explain why language cannot reach there; use concepts to argue why concepts cannot manage it. Every Buddhist sutra is a finger pointing at the moon, and skillful means allows fingers to proliferate infinitely — every type of capacity requires a different finger; different fingers are different sutras. Christianity's Signals evolve through selection; the few that survive are strong. Islam's Signals are compressed through locking; few and hard. Buddhism's Signals proliferate through skillful means; many and soft.

Buddhism is not the domain where the framework's explanatory power is weakest — on the contrary, Buddhism is the most extreme case for the postulates. The Law of the Black Box states that the next-moment direction of Semantic Flow cannot be determined by its current direction plus the incoming Signal; the Law of Dissipation states that Semantic Flow cannot be reduced to Signals without loss. Buddhists read sutras, meditate, and keep precepts; Signals enter abundantly; the postulates operate abundantly; semantics are abundantly produced. "Beyond conceptual discourse" does not mean there is no semantics — it means there is more semantics than language can contain. The Buddhist canon is larger than that of Christianity and Islam combined, yet its degree of institutionalization is the lowest — not because there is little semantics, but because the exit direction of semantics is different. The semantics of Christianity and Islam flow back to the Signal end — they become doctrines, laws, institutions. Buddhist semantics remain at the consciousness end — they become behavior, habits, ways of life, cultural disposition. Less externalization does not mean less output.

The prediction of the Self-Organizing Path: competition among Buddhist traditions operates through a pure market mechanism, producing no heresy adjudication and no doctrinal warfare; but the rate of sectarian turnover is not lower than in the other two religions. The core claims (Four Noble Truths) remain convergent across all traditions; but institutional forms are highly plural, with multiple patterns of information flow coexisting over the long term. Occasional exclusive behavior in history (Nichiren Buddhism, the Gelug school) depends on external political conditions and cannot be sustained through institutionalization. These predictions can be tested through observation.

## **3.5 A Comparison of the Three Paths**

The differences among the three paths do not lie in the amplification elements — Transformation, Functional Convergence, Signal Container Institutionalization, and Mortality Pressure are present in every religion — but in the combination of elements and the dynamic behaviors they produce. Christianity's Open Channel plus no locking equals Signal-end evolution; Islam's Closed Channel plus extreme locking equals stable state; Buddhism's Decentralized Channel plus no locking equals self-organization. All three have competition; the difference lies at the level of competition: Christianity and Islam have market competition plus zero-sum game (heresy adjudication); Buddhism has only pure market competition (no institution of heresy).

Mortality Pressure operates with different intensity across the three paths. Christianity and Islam's Mortality Pressure is ultimate — there is only one chance; this life is the sole testing ground; the driving force is concentrated and urgent. Buddhism's Mortality Pressure is iterative — there is another life; the pressure is distributed across multiple cycles of rebirth; the driving force is dispersed and long-range. This explains why the pace of civilizational expansion in the Gu-King Path and the Locking Path is typically faster than in the Self-Organizing Path.

The three paths also have a deeper dimension of difference: the direction of semantic reflux. The semantics of Christianity and Islam flow back to the Signal end — doctrines, laws, institutions. After semantics have been Transformed, the products are re-encoded as Signal Containers: creeds, canon law, Islamic jurisprudence. This is the Externalization Path — semantics flow from consciousness back to Signal, from individual back to institution. The Externalization Path is easy to observe, easy to quantify, and easy to institutionalize; therefore civilizational expansion is fast, but it is also therefore fragile — destroy the Signal Containers and the institution collapses. Buddhist semantics do not flow back to the Signal end; they remain directly at the consciousness end — becoming behavior, habits, ways of life, cultural disposition. The tea ceremony, calligraphy, mindfulness, attitudes toward Death — all are products of semantics changing how people live, bypassing Signal Containers. This is the Internalization Path — semantics flow from consciousness to behavior, from knowledge to living. The Internalization Path is difficult to observe, difficult to quantify, and difficult to institutionalize; therefore civilizational expansion is slow, but resilience is extremely strong — you can tear down temples, but you cannot strip the already-internalized semantics from a people's daily behavior. This explains the cultural saturation phenomenon of Buddhism in East and Southeast Asia: "to be Thai is to be Buddhist" is not because Thailand has a powerful Buddhist institution — it is because Buddhist semantics have been internalized as the Thai way of life.

One important observation: Signal unification does not equal semantic unification; Signal plurality does not equal semantic fragmentation. Islam has the most unified Signal end, yet the Sunni-Shi'a split still occurred. Buddhism has the most plural Signal end (scriptural editions, languages, and lineages all varying), yet the Four Noble Truths are acknowledged in all traditions. The root of Drift lies in differences in Xin — the same Signal processed by different Xin produces different semantics; semantics are then externalized as different Signal outputs (doctrines, institutions, practices); the split becomes observable here. This supports the predictions of the Semantic Flow Dynamics postulates.

The direction of knowledge flow is another dimension of path difference. The knowledge flow of Christianity and Islam operates in Descending Mode — from beyond the Cognitive Limit to within the Cognitive Limit, direction unidirectional, Channel designated. Christianity's Channel chain is: God → prophets → apostles → Church → believers. Islam's Channel chain is shorter: Allah → Muhammad → the Quran → the Ummah. Buddhism's knowledge flow operates in Ascending Mode — direction completely reversed; the individual through cultivation reaches the Cognitive Limit on their own; the Channel is not designated but individually attainable. In Descending Mode, the gravitational pull of Xin is concentrated on the Channel-holder, easily producing centralized religious institutions. In Ascending Mode, the gravitational pull of Xin is naturally distributed, and institutionalization is relatively limited.

The three religions' response strategies to Death are completely different but functionally equivalent — all are semantic codings of "consciousness terminates, semantics return to zero." Christianity codes Death as a Channel (the door to eternal life); hope strengthens Xin's intensity. Islam codes Death as judgment (the beginning of the final reckoning); Mortality Pressure is converted into a constraining force on present-day behavior. Buddhism codes Death as a link in the cycle of rebirth; Mortality Pressure is converted into a driving force for cultivation. The common effect of the three codings: Death is re-coded from "the termination of semantics" to "the condition for the continuation of semantics"; Death is no longer the terminator of the system but a part of the system.

## **3.6 The Civilizational Arena: Path as Necessary but Not Sufficient Condition**

The three paths explain how the three major religions progressed, but do not explain why some religions walked similar paths and perished. Manichaeism had a Source Claim for Trans-Limit Knowledge; it had Transformation, Functional Convergence, Signal Container Institutionalization, and Mortality Pressure — all elements present. Yet Manichaeism perished. So did Zoroastrianism.

Path as Necessary but Not Sufficient Condition. History is an arena. The path gives you standing to enter the ring; whether you survive depends on the specific conditions in the ring — political protection or political persecution, geographic diffusion or geographic confinement, symbiosis or opposition with structures of power. But paths also have endogenous fragility. Buddhism's extinction on Indian soil cannot be wholly attributed to external conditions — the Self-Organizing Path lacks the institutional capacity to mobilize resistance; this is a structural defect of the path, not merely a historical misfortune. Similarly, Manichaeism had all the elements, but was simultaneously persecuted by the Roman Empire and Sasanian Persia; its Signal Containers were systematically destroyed; the consciousnesses carrying Xin were systematically eliminated. The intergenerational break was not natural — it was forcibly imposed. A complete process-tracing of Manichaeism — at which node, and for what specific historical reasons, did the four elements fail — is a direction for subsequent research; here only the position is marked.

Post-mortem Reckoning applies equally to civilizations. Manichaeism's coffin is closed; history has already rendered its judgment. The coffins of the three major religions are not yet closed, which is why they remain in the ring. What this paper analyzes is the three still in the ring — not because they are more worthy of analysis, but because they are still alive; there is still observable behavior at the Signal end.

# **Chapter 4: Framework Limitations and Challengeable Conditions**

## **4.1 Questions the Framework Does Not Address**

This paper does not address the truth or falsity, social function, psychological function, or political function of religion. This paper only governs how information flows, what paths it takes, and what strategies it uses to resist Drift. The judgments this paper makes are causal-mechanism judgments (for example: Channel closure causes interpretation to become the sole direction of evolution); these judgments are necessary components of the analysis.

The judgments this paper makes are: structural judgments at the epistemological level (for example: human reason has a boundary) and causal-mechanism judgments (for example: Channel closure causes interpretation to become the sole direction of evolution). These judgments are necessary components of the analysis; they do not contradict "not judging the truth or falsity of doctrines."

## **4.2 Structural Limitation: The Semantic End Is Not Quantifiable**

The core mechanism of this paper — Signal Transformation, Semantic Transformation — takes place at the semantic end. The semantic end is not quantifiable. This is not a defect of the framework — it is the structural ceiling of human cognition. To explain what happens internally, language must be used; language itself is the output of semantics; using output to explain the process that produces output is self-referential and must collapse. This limitation applies equally to all human theories, not only to this framework. Any theory claiming to completely quantify "what happens inside the human mind" is either quantifying proxy indicators at the Signal end, or collapsing in self-reference.

This paper chooses to face this ceiling honestly. What can be quantified (the Signal end) is honestly quantified; what cannot be quantified (the semantic end) is honestly acknowledged as unquantifiable. An indirect verification path exists: if the Transformation mechanism holds, the same core claim across different cultures should exhibit a predictable pattern of variation in its institutionalized output — and this can be tested. But direct quantification of semantics themselves is, in principle, impossible.

## **4.3 Challengeable Conditions**

Challenge One: Alternative explanations for the paths. If someone can use a theory that does not involve Signal Transformation — such as pure institutional inertia, power structures, or economic interests — to completely explain the differences among the three dynamic paths, with explanatory power equal to or greater than this paper, then the argument of Chapter 3 is weakened.

Challenge Two: Failure of path predictions. This paper has specified testable predictions for each of the three paths: Christianity will continue to schism, and schism will be followed by competition; Islam's Signal end will maintain high stability, with variation concentrated at the interpretive end; Buddhism's competition operates through a pure market mechanism (no heresy adjudication), with a sectarian turnover rate no lower than in the other two religions. If these predictions are systematically refuted — for example, if Christianity's tendency toward schism ceases, or if Islam undergoes a fundamental rewriting of its Signal end — the description of the corresponding path needs revision. It must be acknowledged that these predictions are too safe — anyone familiar with the history of religion could make similar predictions without the framework. For the framework to prove it is more than terminological packaging, it needs to make precise, differentiated predictions about phenomena that existing theories cannot predict. The most powerful direction is civilizational collision — when religions of different paths meet, what will happen at the Signal end and the semantic end respectively? Such predictions can demonstrate the framework's distinctive explanatory power while being tested against historical and contemporary cases.

Challenge Three: Redundancy in the Five-Stage Model. If someone can demonstrate that one of the five stages can be absorbed by another — for example, that the Cognitive Limit can be subsumed into within the Cognitive Limit as a continuous gradation — then the model's topology needs revision.

Challenge Four: Challenge to the underlying framework. This paper inherits all of Semantic Flow Dynamics' challengeable conditions. If the underlying framework is overturned, the analysis above simultaneously fails. The validity of this paper is bound to the validity of Semantic Flow Dynamics.

## **4.4 Theoretical Counterpoint: What Can Different Frameworks See in the Same Problem?**

The core question of this paper is: how can an extremely small quantity of core religious claims be amplified into entire civilizational structures, and why did the three major religions walk three completely different paths? This question is not unique to the framework — existing theories have each touched on it from different angles. This section does not provide a panoramic literature review; instead, three theories most directly relevant to this paper are selected, with the same phenomenon used as a stress test: examining what each can explain, where each stops, and whether this framework can pick up where each stops.

### **4.4.1 Sperber: The Epidemiology of Representations**

Dan Sperber's (1996) epidemiology of representations is the existing theory closest to this framework. His core claim is nearly isomorphic with this paper's postulates: cultural representations in person-to-person transmission are not copied — they are Transformed. Every transmission involves reconstruction by the receiver. This overlaps substantially with this framework's postulate (the Law of the Black Box: the next-moment direction of Semantic Flow cannot be determined by its current direction plus the incoming Signal) and with Functional Convergence at the group level.

**What Sperber can explain.** He can explain why religious ideas are easy to spread — because certain representations have "attractors" for human cognition, and their structure happens to fit the default tendencies of the human brain. Supernatural agency is a strong attractor: the human brain is naturally inclined to over-attribute agency (Barrett 2004), so representations of the type "conscious supernatural existence" are not easily discarded in transmission — they are instead repeatedly reconstructed. Sperber's framework can explain why all major religions contain claims about supernatural agency — this is the effect of cognitive attractors.

**Where Sperber stops.** He stops at the problem of path divergence. If the transmission of religious ideas is primarily driven by cognitive attractors, all religions should walk similar paths — human cognitive structure is shared; attractors operate similarly across all cultures. But this is not what happened. Christianity walked the Gu-King Path; Islam walked the Locking Path; Buddhism walked the Self-Organizing Path. The divergence of the three paths cannot be explained by cognitive attractors, because all three religions face the same human brains.

A specific case: Islam's Signal-end locking. The principle of the Quran's untranslatability requires all Muslim believers globally to encounter the Arabic original; the isnad system traces the complete transmission Channel of each hadith, recording every intermediate node. Sperber's framework has no tools to describe this strategy of "deliberately suppressing Transformation" — his model assumes that Transformation occurs naturally; what it focuses on is the convergence pattern after Transformation, not the institutional design to intervene in the Transformation process. More critically: even under conditions of extreme Signal-end locking, the Sunni-Shi'a split still occurred. The split is not at the Signal end (both read the same Quran) — it is at the Xin-driven contest for interpretive authority. Sperber's framework does not distinguish between Signal and Xin; therefore it cannot capture the phenomenon of "Signal unified but Xin differs, leading to interpretive fragmentation."

**This framework picks up where Sperber stops.** This framework has two additional tools that solve the problems where Sperber stops. First: "Xin" as a primitive independent of Signal. Sperber's attractors are properties of cognitive structure — they explain which representations are easily accepted, but not why equally easily accepted representations walk completely different paths across different systems. This framework's "Xin" (values and will) provides an explanation for path divergence: the three religions' core claims enter different Xin; Transformation produces different dynamic behaviors. Second: the hard distinction between the Signal end and the semantic end. This distinction allows the framework to describe both "Signal unified but semantics fragmented" and "Signal plural but semantics convergent" (e.g., the Four Noble Truths are acknowledged in all Buddhist traditions) — two phenomena Sperber's framework cannot distinguish.

### **4.4.2 Luhmann: Social Systems Theory**

Niklas Luhmann's social systems theory made an architectural choice exactly opposite to this framework: he excluded consciousness from the social system. In Luhmann's system, the basic unit of social systems is not consciousness but communication. Consciousness belongs to the system's "environment," not its "interior." This was not an oversight — Luhmann knew explicitly that once consciousness is placed inside the system, the system cannot handle the unpredictability of consciousness. He chose to route around it.

**What Luhmann can explain.** He can explain superbly how religion operates as a functional subsystem. The religious system handles the binary code of "transcendence/immanence" — religion converts the indeterminate into determinate communication. Doctrines, rituals, and ecclesiastical hierarchies are all, in Luhmann's framework, communicative forms of the self-reproduction of the religious system. Luhmann can explain why religious institutions have the inertia of self-maintenance — the system continuously self-reproduces through its own operations (autopoiesis).

**Where Luhmann stops.** He stops in two places. First: deep-level upheaval in Xin. Paul's conversion on the road to Damascus — from persecuting Christians to becoming the most important apostle of Christianity — is not the self-reproduction of a communication system; it is an internal event of a consciousness, and this event then changed the direction of the entire system's evolution. Luhmann's framework attributes such events to "environmental perturbation of the system," but the concept of perturbation is too weak — the Paul event is not a perturbation; it is the mutation of a core node; it directly rewrote the entire trajectory of Christianity's shift from an internal Jewish movement to cross-ethnic propagation. In this framework, this is a violent change in Xin's deep layer over an extremely short period — the direction of Semantic Flow suddenly changes (note: the layered structure of Xin into deep, middle, and surface layers is discussed in the upstream natural-language paper, which has not entered the upstream formalization document); the effect is transmitted through the Positive Feedback Loop from the individual to the entire system.

Second: the Death of a civilization. Before the dissolution of the Soviet Union, all communicative subsystems were operating normally — the political system, the legal system, the educational system, the military system. According to Luhmann's framework, as long as communication continues, the system is self-reproducing. But the Soviet Union died. Luhmann's framework can retrospectively describe "the system's self-reproduction stopped," but it struggles to explain why the system stopped self-reproducing under conditions where communication was still continuing. This framework's explanation is: the Signal Containers were intact, but the Xin driving the Signals had emptied — the gap between the direction of Semantic Flow and what the Signal Containers claimed continued to expand; the Positive Feedback Loop began to idle (Loop Instability). No one was genuinely operating with Xin aligned with Marxism-Leninism; communication became idling ritual. Signal end normal, semantic end zeroed — Luhmann does not distinguish between these two ends, so this process is invisible to him.

**This framework picks up where Luhmann stops.** The fundamental divergence between this framework and Luhmann is an architectural choice: he excludes consciousness from the system to gain descriptive power at the system level; this framework places consciousness at the center to gain explanatory power at the dynamic level. The two are not mutually exclusive — Luhmann can see the patterns of communication; this framework can see the driving force behind the patterns. Specifically applied to religious analysis: Luhmann can describe the communicative structures of the three major religions; this framework can explain why these three communicative structures walked completely different dynamic paths — because the Xin driving communication differs; the qualitative properties of Xin differ; and the strategies Xin uses to handle Drift differ.

### **4.4.3 Dawkins: Memetics**

Richard Dawkins' (1976) concept of the meme is the most familiar framework for cultural transmission in the popular discourse. The core presupposition of memetics is: cultural units can be copied like genes; copying involves mutation; mutations undergo selection pressure; memes with greater fitness survive. This analogy superficially resembles this paper's Gu-King Path.

**Where memetics stops.** It stops at the foundations. The basic presupposition of memetics — that cultural units can be copied — is directly denied by this framework's postulates. Signals can be copied (Shannon's domain); semantics cannot. The same Quran read by a billion people has identical Signals but varied semantics. Memetics treats semantics as Signals; it treats Transformation as defects in copying; it walks the wrong direction from the very starting point.

Specifically applied to the explanation of the three paths: memetics can only handle the Gu-King Path (variation + selection = evolution); for the Locking Path and the Self-Organizing Path, it is nearly helpless. Islam's locking strategy is not "high-fidelity copying of a meme" — it is a deliberate institutional design whose purpose is to suppress variation at the Signal end. But suppressing the Signal end does not equal suppressing the semantic end. The Sunni-Shi'a split can only be explained in memetics as a "copying error," but in reality there was no error at the Signal end — both read the same Quran; the split occurred behind the Signal. Buddhism's Self-Organizing Path is even further outside memetics' scope: skillful means fundamentally cancels the possibility of zero-sum competition — every practitioner's cultivation is unique; the vast scale of the Tripiṭaka accommodates almost all variation; there is no mechanism of "selection pressure eliminating maladaptive versions." Buddhism does not eliminate — it accommodates. Dawkins' model of "variation + selection" is completely ineffective for Buddhism.

**This framework picks up where Dawkins stops.** This framework's replacement of memetics is not a revision but a replacement of basic presuppositions. The fundamental behavior of culture is not copying — it is Transformation. Stability is not the result of high-fidelity copying; it is the Functional Convergence of large quantities of Transformation at the Signal end. The difference among the three paths is not a difference in mutation rate — it is a difference in the strategies Xin uses to handle Drift: the Gu-King Path (permitting variation, using competition to filter); the Locking Path (suppressing Signal-end variation, pushing Drift to the interpretive end); the Self-Organizing Path (permitting variation, making no adjudication). Memetics has vocabulary for only the first path.

### **4.4.4 Summary Counterpoint**

Each of the three theories has touched on a part of this paper's question. Sperber saw Transformation but lacked tools to distinguish between the Signal end and the semantic end, and lacked "Xin" to explain path divergence. Luhmann saw the system's self-reproduction but excluded consciousness, unable to see how deep-level upheaval in Xin and idling of the Positive Feedback Loop rewrite the system from within. Dawkins saw competition and selection but his basic presupposition is wrong — culture does not copy, culture Transforms.

This framework's position is not "compatible with all of them" but: where each encounters the wall at the Signal end or the system end, this framework picks up from the other side of the wall. Sperber's attractors explain which representations are easy to transmit; this framework explains why different paths emerge after transmission. Luhmann's communication systems describe the institutional forms of religion; this framework explains why institutional forms collapse from within or are reorganized from without. Dawkins' selection pressure describes one mechanism of the Gu-King Path; this framework explains why there are also the two completely different paths of the Locking Path and the Self-Organizing Path.

Building one's own framework is not the problem; being non-testable is. This paper has specified testable predictions for each of the three paths (3.2–3.4), but it must be honestly acknowledged: these predictions come from the generative cases of the framework; the framework itself was induced from these observations; using predictions from generative cases to verify the framework risks circular reasoning. Real testing must come from independent cases — phenomena not considered when the framework was built. If someone can use Sperber, Luhmann, or other existing frameworks to give equally precise explanations for these predictions, then this framework is a redundant hypothesis that should be replaced by a more parsimonious theory. No such alternative has been seen so far, but the absence of alternatives does not equal the correctness of this framework.

This paper is the generative case of Semantic Flow Dynamics — the framework grew from this observation, and this paper uses the framework to revisit the observation. This is a normal process of theory-building, but it also means this paper cannot serve as verification for the framework. Verification must come from independent cases — phenomena not used when the framework was built. # **Conclusion**

Religion is the most enduring information flow system in human civilization. This paper has demonstrated religion's operational logic through the Five-Stage Model and three dynamic paths.

The Five-Stage Model is grounded in the structural facts of cognition: values and will prior to information reception (Xin); the domain of verifiable knowledge (within the Cognitive Limit); the boundary of human reason (the Cognitive Limit); the unverifiable domain (beyond the Cognitive Limit); and the physical termination of consciousness (Death). These do not depend on any religious doctrine — they are the structure of cognition itself.

The three major religions share the basic elements of amplification — Signal Transformation, Functional Convergence, Signal Container Institutionalization, Mortality Pressure — but the combination of elements produced three completely different dynamic paths. Christianity walks the Gu-King Path: Open Channel, no Signal locking, repeated schism and collision, evolution of survivors. Islam walks the Locking Path: Closed Channel, extreme Signal locking, variation suppressed, stable state formed. Buddhism walks the Self-Organizing Path: Decentralized Channel, no locking, only pure market competition, no zero-sum game (heresy adjudication). All three have competition; the difference lies in the structure of competition: the Abrahamic religions have market plus zero-sum; Buddhism has only market.

Path differences are not doctrinal differences — doctrinal differences are surface — but structural differences in information flow. Different paths produce different civilizational forms. The semantics of Christianity and Islam flow back to the Signal end, following the Externalization Path — producing doctrines, laws, and institutions; expansion is fast but dependent on Signal Containers. Buddhist semantics remain at the consciousness end, following the Internalization Path — directly entering behavior and ways of life; expansion is slow but resilience is extremely strong. Buddhism is not the domain where the framework's explanatory power is weakest — it is the most extreme case for the postulates: the greatest semantic output, the lowest Signal reflux rate; "unspeakable" does not mean there is no semantics; it means there is more semantics than language can contain.

The non-quantifiability of the semantic end is the structural ceiling of human cognition; self-reference must collapse; this applies equally to all theories. This paper chooses to face this ceiling honestly rather than pretend it does not exist. What can be quantified is honestly quantified; what cannot be quantified is honestly acknowledged as unquantifiable.

This paper's contribution is not to judge the truth or falsity of religion, but to provide a framework for analyzing religion without needing to discuss doctrine — a framework anchored in structural facts rather than self-constructed definitions. Three Religions × Five Dimensions is the compressed table of this analysis; the Five-Stage Model is its structural foundation; the three dynamic paths are its core findings; Semantic Flow Dynamics is its underlying tool.

If religion is an information flow system, then the tools for analyzing it should be the dynamics of information flow, not a contest between the positions of theology or atheism. This paper is the first step in this direction — and the generative case of Semantic Flow Dynamics. The framework grew from this observation; this paper used the framework to revisit the observation. The next step is independent verification: make precise predictions with the framework about phenomena not considered when it was built, and then see whether the predictions hold. The generative case demonstrates explanatory power; independent verification determines the framework's survival or extinction.
