## Abstract

Large language models (LLMs) are the first information systems in human history forced to simultaneously ingest knowledge at every scale level. Physics, biology, history, law, everyday conversation—all compressed into a single set of parameters. What humanity spent thousands of years carefully keeping separate has been crammed wholesale into one architecture. This creates a structural dilemma.

This paper proposes the **Information Trilemma**: in any information system, when Scale remains open, logical consistency and factual truthfulness cannot be simultaneously satisfied. The three vertices are **Scale** (simultaneously accommodating sufficiently complete knowledge systems at multiple distinct scale levels), **Logic** (internal inferential consistency), and **Truth** (correspondence with verifiable reality at a specific scale level).

The core reason the trilemma holds: the fundamental rules of reality exist across scale levels, Logic holds across scale levels, but concrete phenomena are the Combinatorial Manifestation of rules at specific scale levels—and Combinatorial Manifestation changes with scale level. This is an observed fact, independent of emergence, chaos, or nonlinearity. Rule-weight scale-level dependence alone is sufficient for the trilemma to hold. The constraint operates not at the level of rules (a successful unified field theory would not affect the trilemma), but at the combinatorial layer between rules and phenomena. The trilemma does not explain why multiple distinct scale levels exist, nor why Combinatorial Manifestation changes with scale level—just as the second law of thermodynamics does not explain why entropy increases. It starts from an observed fact and derives a constraint.

Because LLM training data naturally spans a vast number of scale levels, the trilemma becomes an engineering reality rather than a theoretical curiosity. Hallucinations are not bugs—they are a structural cost. But "structural" does not mean "unimprovable." RAG, tool calling, and domain fine-tuning all reduce hallucinations by sacrificing Scale in exchange for compatible Logic and Truth—the same strategy humanity uses when it divides knowledge into disciplines.

This paper positions itself as an empirical heuristic, not a formal theorem—in the family of the CAP theorem and the Mundell-Fleming trilemma, but grounded in observed facts rather than formal proof. Its value lies not in proving impossibility but in providing a trade-off framework for data cleaning and model design: you are making a two-out-of-three choice. Be clear about which vertex you are sacrificing this time.

**Keywords:** information trilemma, scale level, combinatorial manifestation, LLM, data cleaning, hallucination

# Part One: The Problem—LLMs Swallowed Every Scale Level

---

## I. LLMs Are the First System to Actually Do This

Human knowledge systems have always been compartmentalized. Physics is divided by scale level, medicine by specialty, engineering by domain, history by period. Every functional knowledge system begins by drawing a boundary—"I only handle what falls within here"—and only then achieves logical consistency and correspondence with reality.

Science is divided into disciplines not because humans are too lazy to unify, but because each discipline is an operation that locks onto a scale level. Every attempt at unification—grand unified theories, theories of everything—has taken decades and produced no results, because you are trying to keep Scale open while preserving Logic and Truth simultaneously.

LLMs broke this convention. Training a large language model means feeding it Wikipedia, textbooks, papers, news, forums, code, legal documents, and fiction all at once, under a single unified architecture. This is not a design choice—it is an operational necessity. To make a model that can answer any question, you must feed it data from every domain.

The result: what humanity spent thousands of years carefully keeping separate has been crammed wholesale into a single set of parameters.

---

## II. The Proposition

**In any information system, when Scale remains open, logical consistency and factual truthfulness cannot be simultaneously satisfied.**

This is not an engineering "not yet achieved"—it is a structural "cannot be achieved"—observed as a structural constraint, not proven as an impossibility. The limitation does not stem from insufficient resources or inadequate algorithms; it stems from the structure of the input data itself.

---

## III. Definitions

**Combinatorial Manifestation:** The overall effect of a rule set interacting under specific conditions, C(R). Combinatorial Manifestation is directly observable and measurable—the combination of phenomena observable under specific conditions. The rule set remains constant across conditions, but the relative weights of rules and their modes of interaction change with conditions, causing Combinatorial Manifestation to differ across conditions.

**Scale Level:** The equivalence class of conditions that produce the same Combinatorial Manifestation, denoted s. All conditions producing the same Combinatorial Manifestation belong to the same Scale Level; conditions producing different Combinatorial Manifestations belong to different Scale Levels. When written C(R, s), s names "the set of conditions that produce the same Combinatorial Manifestation." Scale Levels are not given a priori—they are induced from observable differences in Combinatorial Manifestation.

**Logic:** Internal inferential consistency. The same reasoning rules remain non-contradictory throughout.

**Truth:** Correspondence with verifiable reality at a specific Scale Level. Maps onto actually observable outcomes.

**Knowledge:** Structured understanding of reality at a specific Scale Level, recorded as text. Knowledge does not equal reality—it is a lossy record of reality (see Section X). For LLMs, Knowledge is the text in the training data.

**Scale:** Simultaneously accommodating sufficiently complete knowledge systems at multiple distinct Scale Levels.

Scale is not data volume. Knowledge at each Scale Level has its own combinatorial logic, its own empirical parameters, its own emergent phenomena, and requires sufficient volume of knowledge to support effective operation. Greater Scale means maintaining more local knowledge systems simultaneously. For LLMs, Scale is the diversity of knowledge in training data—how many distinct Scale Levels are spanned.

# Part Two: The Core Argument—Combinatorial Manifestation Changes with Scale Level

---

## IV. Logic Is Cross-Scale, Rules Are Cross-Scale, Combinatorial Manifestation Is Not

Logic is cross-scale. The four fundamental forces are cross-scale too—gravity, electromagnetism, the strong nuclear force, and the weak nuclear force all exist at every Scale Level. The rules have not changed; Logic has not changed.

But any concrete phenomenon in reality is not the expression of a single rule—it is the combined effect of multiple rules at a specific Scale Level. Why is water liquid? Electromagnetism dominates intermolecular interactions, quantum mechanics determines electron orbitals, gravity is negligible at this Scale Level. Move to a neutron star: the same four forces are present, but the weights of the strong nuclear force and gravity are entirely different, and matter behaves entirely differently.

The rules have not changed, but the weights of the rules change with Scale Level. The mode of combination changes with Scale Level.

Truth is not the rules. Truth is the Combinatorial Manifestation of the rules at a specific Scale Level. This mode of combination cannot be derived logically from the rules alone—it can only be observed from reality at that Scale Level.

---

## V. This Argument Does Not Depend on Emergence

Combinatorial Manifestation changing with Scale Level does not require emergence to explain. Electromagnetic and gravitational fields have nearly the same structure (both follow the inverse-square law), yet electromagnetism is approximately 10³⁶ times stronger than gravity, and electric charges cancel while mass does not. These two simple differences alone are sufficient to make the same rules produce entirely different Combinatorial Manifestations at the atomic Scale Level and the astronomical Scale Level.

No chaos, no nonlinearity, no complex systems theory required. Combinatorial Manifestation changing with Scale Level is a more fundamental observed fact than emergence. Emergence is an aggravating condition—it makes differences in Combinatorial Manifestation more dramatic—but it is not a necessary condition. Even without emergence, rule-weight Scale Level dependence alone is sufficient for the Information Trilemma to hold.

This matters because emergence is itself a contested concept—some argue it can be reduced. The Information Trilemma does not need to fight that battle. Its premise rests only on observed facts that no one can deny.

---

## VI. Why Combinatorial Manifestation Differs Across Scale Levels—Why the Trilemma Holds

Combinatorial Manifestation differs across Scale Levels. This is not a theory—it is an observed fact. The Information Trilemma does not need to explain why it differs; it only needs to observe that it does. Scale Level is defined by differences in Combinatorial Manifestation (see Section III). The Information Trilemma does not explain why multiple distinct Scale Levels exist—just as the second law of thermodynamics does not need to explain why entropy increases; it observes that entropy does increase, then derives from that fact. This is a boundary deliberately drawn by this paper, not an omission.

The derivation path:

One: Truth at Scale Level s₁ depends on the Combinatorial Manifestation of the rules at s₁, C(s₁).

Two: Truth at Scale Level s₂ depends on the Combinatorial Manifestation of the rules at s₂, C(s₂).

Three: C(s₁) ≠ C(s₂). This is an observed fact.

Four: The logical framework established at s₁ is based on C(s₁). When applied to s₂, because C(s₂) ≠ C(s₁), it is not guaranteed to correspond with Truth at s₂.

Five: When Scale simultaneously covers s₁ and s₂, unified Logic and Truth at all Scale Levels cannot simultaneously be satisfied.

For LLMs, this means: training data simultaneously contains Knowledge at s₁, s₂, s₃ through sₙ—each true within its own Scale Level, but their Logics are mutually incompatible. A model using one set of parameters to fit all this data must inevitably sacrifice either logical consistency or factual truthfulness somewhere.

---

## VII. Distinction from Unified Field Theory

The Information Trilemma is not saying the rules cannot be unified—it is saying Combinatorial Manifestation cannot be captured by a unified logical framework.

Even if someone tomorrow found a unified field theory—a beautiful set of equations unifying the four forces—to calculate the boiling point of water with those equations you would still need to substitute boundary conditions at a specific Scale Level, make approximations, and choose cutoffs. The equations are unified, but the path from equations to concrete phenomena is different at every Scale Level. A unified field theory unifies the rules, not the Combinatorial Manifestation.

The constraint of the Information Trilemma operates not at the level of rules but at the combinatorial layer between rules and phenomena. Even when rules are fully unified, Combinatorial Manifestation still cannot bridge across Scale Levels. This means the constraint of the Information Trilemma is deeper than intuition suggests.

---

## VIII. The Trilemma Self-Reinforces

Knowledge within each Scale Level can each be internally logically perfect. Planetary mechanics is self-consistent; quantum mechanics is self-consistent. But at the moment of crossing Scale Levels, logical consistency breaks—at the singularity of a black hole, general relativity says density is infinite, quantum mechanics says this is meaningless. Both are right; together they are contradictory.

The more complete and rigorous the Knowledge at each Scale Level, the sharper the logical conflict when crossing Scale Levels—because each has developed a complete system, and the foundational assumptions of these systems fail at other Scale Levels.

The better each of two vertices performs, the more thoroughly the third collapses.

For LLMs: the higher the quality of training data and the more complete the coverage within each domain, the more apparent the contradictions in cross-domain reasoning. Not because the data is bad—because good data fights with good data.

# Part Three: The Specific Toxicity of Training Data

---

## IX. Every Domain's Data Carries Its Own Poison

LLM training data comes from every domain, and every domain's data carries cross-Scale Level contradictions—not a data quality problem, but the structure of reality itself.

Physics data is internally contradictory across Scale Levels. General relativity and quantum mechanics are each internally consistent but mutually incompatible. The model learns both frameworks, with no data telling it when to use which—because humanity has not figured that out either.

Biology data describes a system that does not optimize for logical consistency. The same functional need is solved in completely different ways across species; at every level from genes to organism, higher-level phenomena cannot be predicted from the lower; and biological organisms are full of evolutionary redundancies and contradictions. Asking a model to learn logically consistent Knowledge from this data is asking the data to provide something it never had.

Humanities data carries not only cross-Scale Level contradictions but also "Truth" shaped by power. The same fundamental rules (people need to eat, people die) combine into completely different institutions across different times and places, and the mode of combination derives heavily from deliberate construction by power. The model has no capacity to distinguish which parts are observed facts and which are narratives constructed under specific power structures.

On the surface these are different problems in different domains; underneath it is the same structure. It is not that physics data has physics poison and biology has biology poison—there is only one kind of poison, and each domain merely contracted it from a different angle. The difference lies in the dimensionality of the combinatorial variables: physics combinations arise naturally and have lower dimensionality; humanities combinations are shaped by power and have extremely high dimensionality. The constraint strength of the Information Trilemma increases accordingly.

# Part Four: Aggravating Conditions—The Defects of Knowledge Itself

---

Combinatorial Manifestation changing with Scale Level is a sufficient condition for the Information Trilemma. Even if Knowledge were perfectly intact, as long as Combinatorial Manifestation differs across Scale Levels, the trilemma holds.

But the Knowledge LLMs ingest is not perfectly intact. The defects of Knowledge are observable facts that make the constraints of the Information Trilemma more severe.

---

## X. Knowledge Is Lossy

Knowledge does not completely reflect reality. This is an observed fact.

The modes of loss are structural: time is discarded—dynamic processes become static records; the continuous transitions between Scale Levels are discarded—replaced by a discrete system of academic disciplines; the complete details of Combinatorial Manifestation are discarded—replaced by empirical parameters and magic numbers; context is discarded—replaced by apparently universal propositions.

LLM training data is the textualization of this lossy Knowledge. Every layer discards something, but the output of every layer appears complete.

---

## XI. The Specific Defects of Knowledge

**Irreproducibility.** A large quantity of research results cannot be independently replicated. The reproducibility crisis is not a problem confined to individual fields—it is a structural characteristic of the knowledge production process. Training a model on this data and expecting it to satisfy the Information Trilemma is not computation—it is wishful thinking.

**Inexplicable empirical parameters.** Theories that actually work are often full of empirical parameters and magic numbers. An engineer dials in a working parameter; ask them why 0.7 and not 0.8, and the answer is "I tried it." Papers write "empirically determined"—translated into plain language: "I don't know why but it works." The Knowledge that actually works is precisely the Knowledge least conformant to "logically rigorous" standards.

**Contradictory conclusions.** Different studies reach opposite conclusions on the same question. Not because someone did it wrong, but because different experimental conditions constitute different Scale Levels, and Truth at different Scale Levels can legitimately differ.

**Post-hoc rationalization.** A large amount of theoretical explanation is found after the fact, not derived beforehand. Why does this learning rate work in deep learning? Why set dropout at 0.5? Why is ReLU better than sigmoid? The answers are mostly post-hoc attribution, not prior deduction.

Differences in Combinatorial Manifestation are the structural constraint; Knowledge defects are the stacking aggravation. Both act simultaneously on LLM training data.

---

# Part Five: Specific Constraints on LLMs

---

## XII. LLMs Are a Re-Compression of Incomplete Knowledge

Knowledge already incompletely reflects reality. LLMs train on this incomplete Knowledge, performing another compression on an already imperfect foundation. Every layer discards something, but every layer's output appears complete and confident.

What LLM training requires is not "true and logically rigorous data" but data that covers the diversity of human language and Knowledge. Scale here is not just "large volume" but "large diversity"—spanning a vast number of different Scale Levels. To cover the breadth of human Knowledge, one must include data with conflicting viewpoints, imprecise Logic, and even factual errors—because that is what real-world Knowledge actually looks like.

---

## XIII. The Structural Limitations of the Algorithm

The current dominant architecture (Transformer) is fundamentally performing statistical correlation—not logical reasoning. The "Logic" it learns is patterns induced from vast amounts of text, not deduction from axioms. Statistical induction and logical deduction are two fundamentally different cognitive mechanisms; forcing one architecture to do both simultaneously inevitably involves compromise somewhere.

Modeling can partially circumvent this limitation—physics simulation, weather forecasting, protein folding; these models have time, causality, and continuous evolution. But all modeling is Scale Level-specific. What modeling can do is recover causal structure in closed systems with known rules and known Scale Levels. What modeling cannot do is decide what model to use—when to apply Newtonian mechanics versus when to switch to relativity. That judgment is not something modeling can solve. Modeling's successes do not represent breakthroughs of the Information Trilemma; they represent abandonment of the Scale vertex.

---

## XIV. Hallucinations Are Structural, but Manageable

LLMs systematically favor Knowledge combinations with large Scale, apparently rigorous Logic, but uncontrollable Truth.

The essence of genuinely useful Knowledge is often hidden in the practitioner's intuition, feel, and failure experiences that never made it into papers. These things have never become text. LLMs can only learn Knowledge that has been recorded as text—and that Knowledge is already incomplete.

At the same time, irreproducible experimental results, overturned conclusions, and mutually contradictory meta-analyses in training data are textually indistinguishable from reliable Knowledge. LLMs have no capacity to distinguish them, because doing so requires not text analysis but interaction with reality at a specific Scale Level—and LLMs have only text—not the world.

LLM hallucinations are not bugs. They are the inevitable cost of using a unified architecture to span incommensurable Scale Levels. Natural language has never carried Scale Level markers—the context that Knowledge loses when recorded as text includes precisely "the Scale Level at which this proposition holds" (see Section X). LLMs learn from this text and their outputs inherit the "apparently universal" form. The Information Trilemma necessarily leads to ¬Truth(s) at some Scale Levels, but outputs carry no Scale Level conditions, so hallucinations present as confident errors rather than marked uncertainty.

But "structural" does not mean "unimprovable." Hallucination rates have been declining. The way they decline precisely confirms the Information Trilemma: humanity avoids the Information Trilemma by dividing into disciplines—constraining Scale; LLMs avoid the Information Trilemma by task-specialization, domain-separation, and external verification—RAG temporarily narrows Scale to retrieved documents, tool calling delegates Truth judgment to verifiable systems, domain fine-tuning locks a general model onto a specific Scale Level. Different strategies, same logic: sacrificing Scale to gain compatible Logic and Truth.

The Information Trilemma does not say hallucinations cannot be reduced; it says every method for reducing hallucinations sacrifices Scale. You cannot reduce hallucinations to zero while maintaining fully open Scale.

---

## XV. Data Cleaning as a Two-Out-of-Three Choice

Data cleaning is making trade-offs between the three vertices. This is the most direct engineering application of the Information Trilemma.

Tighten cleaning rules, and logical consistency goes up—but you will cut data that "looks contradictory but actually reflects Truth at different Scale Levels." Loosen cleaning rules, and diversity is preserved—but noise and contradictions enter. There is no set of rules that can simultaneously maximize all three vertices. You are making a two-out-of-three choice.

Specific pain points:

The same medical question: a 2010 meta-analysis and a 2024 conclusion contradict each other. Keep which? Keep both—Logic is contradictory. Keep only the newer—you may discard Truth that still holds under specific conditions. Perhaps the 2010 conclusion remains correct for specific populations at specific dosages. This cannot be solved with better rules, because "better" is itself Scale Level-dependent.

The same engineering parameter: the textbook says use 0.7; a senior engineer's blog says use 0.85 under specific conditions. Remove the blog, and you lose practical Knowledge. Keep the blog, and you introduce data contradicting the textbook. And those "specific conditions" are often not explicitly recorded—they live in the engineer's intuition, and never became structured text.

Cross-cultural data is more troublesome. The same legal concept has different meanings across jurisdictions; the same historical event has different narratives in different countries' textbooks. When cleaning, whose version do you use? Choose one, and you sacrifice Truth at a specific Scale Level; keep all, and Logic is inconsistent.

The priority order between the three vertices is itself not fixed. A magic number in an engineer's pocket is not normally considered authoritative—but when supplies are short, it becomes life-saving. Even if an information system could trade off between the three vertices, it does not know which trade-off to prioritize at this moment. That judgment requires understanding the current context—and context is a Scale Level question, which loops back to the Information Trilemma's own constraints.

The takeaway for those doing data cleaning: you are not searching for an optimal solution—you are making a two-out-of-three choice. Think clearly about which vertex this task requires you to sacrifice, and sacrifice it consciously, rather than pretending you can have all three.

# Part Six: Boundary Conditions and Structured Description

---

## XVI. Where the Trilemma Does Not Apply

Fractals are structures where the same rules produce the same Combinatorial Manifestation at every Scale Level—the same pattern repeating infinitely. Fractals can be self-consistent across Scale Levels because they purchase Scale Level invariance by radically abandoning complexity.

Geometry is cross-Scale Level, logically consistent, and true within its assumptions. Geometry can hold across Scale Levels because it does not handle the combination of forces—it only describes the structure of space, not how things within that space interact.

The applicable condition of the Information Trilemma is: Combinatorial Manifestation changes with Scale Level. The criterion is interaction—with interaction there is combination, combination can change with Scale Level, and the Information Trilemma applies. Pure structural, non-interacting formal systems (geometry, fractals, pure mathematics) can be self-consistent across Scale Levels. Once actual phenomena are involved—physical, biological, humanistic—the Information Trilemma holds.

For LLMs, this draws a line: a model can be cross-Scale Level self-consistent in purely formal reasoning (mathematics, Logic), but once empirical Knowledge is involved, the constraints of the Information Trilemma activate.

## XVII. Structured Description

The following is not a formal proof but a structured statement of observed facts and the derivation path.

```
Definitions:
  Scale = simultaneously covering multiple scale levels s₁, s₂, ..., sₘ,
          with a sufficiently complete knowledge subset K(sⱼ) at each scale level
  Logic = ∀ propositions p, q ∈ System: ¬(p ∧ ¬p)
  Truth(s) = the output of System corresponds to verifiable reality at scale level s

Observed Facts:
  F1: Fundamental rules R exist across scale levels
  F2: Concrete phenomena = the combinatorial manifestation C(R, s) of R
      at specific scale level s
  F3: C(R, s₁) ≠ C(R, s₂) when s₁ ≠ s₂
      (observed fact, independent of emergence)

Derivation:
  From F2: Truth(s) depends on C(R, s)
  From F3: C(R, s₁) ≠ C(R, s₂)
  ∴ The logical framework L₁ established at s₁ is based on C(R, s₁)
    When L₁ is applied to s₂, because C(R, s₂) ≠ C(R, s₁),
    Truth(s₂) holding is not guaranteed
  ∴ When Scale is open, unified Logic and Truth at all scale levels
    cannot simultaneously be satisfied

  Note: "not guaranteed" here is an induction from observed facts,
  not a proof of mathematical impossibility.
  The trilemma is an empirical heuristic, not a theorem.

Boundary Condition:
  If ∀s₁, s₂: C(R, s₁) = C(R, s₂)
  (Combinatorial Manifestation does not change with Scale Level, e.g., fractals),
  the trilemma does not apply.

Aggravating Condition (Knowledge Defects):
  K(s) incompletely reflects C(R, s) (observed fact)
  ∴ Even within a single scale level, Logic and Truth are not guaranteed
    to simultaneously hold
  Differences in Combinatorial Manifestation + Knowledge defects both act,
  making the trilemma's constraints more severe

LLM Constraints:
  LLM training data D = ∪ K(sⱼ), j = 1, ..., m
  LLM fits D using unified parameters θ
  By the trilemma: there exists no θ such that ∀s: Truth(s) holds
  under unified Logic
  Avoidance strategies (RAG, tool calling, domain fine-tuning) =
    constraining Scale to gain Logic ∧ Truth
```

## XVIII. Relationship to Other Trilemmas

Trilemmas form a family. CAP theorem says distributed systems must trade off among consistency, availability, and partition tolerance—choose two. Mundell-Fleming says capital mobility, fixed exchange rates, and independent monetary policy—choose two. This paper's Information Trilemma says Scale, Logic, and Truth—choose two.

The structure is the same: three good things cannot all be had simultaneously; systems have an inherent trade-off ceiling; it is not a resource problem but a structural one.

The difference lies in the source and hardness of the constraints. CAP has a formal proof; Mundell-Fleming has model backing. This paper's Information Trilemma is grounded in observed facts—an empirical heuristic, not a theorem. It is broader than CAP and Mundell-Fleming (applicable to all empirical knowledge systems), but softer (lacking formal proof). This paper does not pursue formal proof. The value of an empirical heuristic in engineering practice does not depend on formalization—engineers need a framework that guides trade-offs, not a theorem that passes peer review.

---

## XIX. Range of Application and Limitations

**Applicable:** Any knowledge system in which Combinatorial Manifestation changes with Scale Level. LLM training data naturally falls into this category, spanning physics, biology, humanities, and all other empirical knowledge domains.

**Not applicable:** Pure formal systems in which Combinatorial Manifestation does not change with Scale Level—fractals, geometry, pure mathematics. LLM performance in these domains is not constrained by the Information Trilemma.

**Limitations:** The Information Trilemma does not predict which specific component will fail. It does not judge the priority order of the three vertices—priority order is Scale Level-dependent, varying with context and supply-demand. It does not provide solutions—it describes structural constraints, not problems that can be overcome by engineering means. The Information Trilemma does not explain the nature of Scale Level, nor why Combinatorial Manifestation changes with Scale Level—it only observes that it does, and derives constraints from that observation. The Information Trilemma itself is also Knowledge, and also incomplete.
