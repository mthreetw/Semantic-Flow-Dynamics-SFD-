# The Information Trilemma

*資訊不可能三角*

Cheng Yu Huang / 黃正宇

## Abstract

Large language models (LLMs) are the first information systems in human history forced to simultaneously ingest knowledge across all Scale Levels. Physics, biology, history, law, everyday conversation — all compressed into a single set of parameters. What humanity spent thousands of years carefully separating has been crammed wholesale into one architecture. This creates a structural dilemma.

This paper proposes the Information Trilemma: in any information system, when Scale remains open, logical consistency and factual Truth cannot be simultaneously satisfied. The three vertices are: Scale (simultaneously accommodating adequately sufficient knowledge systems across multiple distinct Scale Levels), Logic (the internal inferential consistency of the system), and Truth (correspondence with verifiable reality at a specific Scale Level).

The core reason the trilemma holds: the fundamental rules of reality exist and are invariant across Scale Levels; Logic holds across Scale Levels; but concrete phenomena are the Combinatorial Manifestation of rules at a specific Scale Level, and Combinatorial Manifestation changes with Scale Level. This is an observed fact — it does not depend on contested concepts such as emergence, chaos, or nonlinearity. The scale-dependence of rule weights alone is sufficient to establish the trilemma. The trilemma's constraint lies not at the rule layer (even a successful unified field theory would not affect it) but at the combinatorial layer between rules and phenomena. The trilemma does not explain why multiple distinct Scale Levels exist, nor why Combinatorial Manifestation varies with Scale Level — just as the Second Law of Thermodynamics does not explain why entropy increases. It derives constraints from observed facts.

LLMs' training data naturally spans a large number of Scale Levels, so the trilemma becomes not just a theoretical question but an engineering reality. Hallucination is not a bug — it is a structural cost. But "structural" does not mean "unimprovable" — RAG, tool-calling, and domain fine-tuning all trade Scale for the compatibility of Logic and Truth, the same strategy as dividing knowledge into academic disciplines.

This paper positions itself as an empirical rule, not a formalized theorem — belonging to the same family as the CAP theorem and the Mundell-Fleming trilemma, but grounded in observed fact rather than formal proof. Its value lies not in proving impossibility but in providing a trade-off framework for data cleaning and model design: you are making a two-out-of-three choice; be clear about which vertex you are sacrificing this time.

**Keywords:** Information Trilemma, Scale Level, Combinatorial Manifestation, LLM, data cleaning, hallucination

# Part I: The Problem — LLMs Have Ingested All Scale Levels

---

## 1. LLMs Are the First System to Actually Do This

Human knowledge systems have always been compartmentalized. Physics is divided by Scale Level, medicine by specialty, engineering by domain, history by period. Every functional knowledge system first draws a boundary — "I only handle what is inside here" — and only then achieves internal Logic and correspondence with reality.

The division of science into disciplines is not because humans are too lazy to unify — it is because each discipline is an operation that locks in a Scale Level. Once you attempt to unify — grand unified theory, theory of everything — decades of effort yield nothing, because you are trying to keep Scale open while preserving Logic and Truth.

LLMs broke this convention. To train a large language model, you feed it Wikipedia, textbooks, papers, news, forums, code, legal documents, fiction — everything, learned through a single unified architecture. This is not a design choice; it is a practical necessity. For a model to answer any question, it must be fed data from all domains.

The result: what humanity spent thousands of years carefully keeping separate has been crammed wholesale into a single set of parameters.

---

## 2. The Proposition

**In any information system, when Scale remains open, logical consistency and factual Truth cannot be simultaneously satisfied.**

This is not an engineering "not yet achieved" — it is a structural "cannot be achieved" — observed as a structural constraint, not proven as an impossibility. The constraint does not arise from insufficient resources or poor algorithms; it arises from the structure of the input data itself.

---

## 3. Definitions

**Combinatorial Manifestation:** The overall effect of a rule set interacting under specific conditions. Combinatorial Manifestation is a directly observable, measurable quantity — the combination of phenomena observable under specific conditions. The rule set is invariant across conditions, but the relative weights and interaction modes of each rule change with conditions, causing Combinatorial Manifestation to differ across conditions.

**Scale Level:** The equivalence class of conditions that produce the same Combinatorial Manifestation. All conditions producing the same Combinatorial Manifestation belong to the same Scale Level; conditions producing different Combinatorial Manifestations belong to different Scale Levels. Scale Levels are not given a priori — they are induced from observable differences in Combinatorial Manifestation.

**Logic:** The internal inferential consistency of a system. The same set of inferential rules holds without contradiction from beginning to end.

**Truth:** Correspondence with verifiable reality at a specific Scale Level. Capable of mapping to actually observable results.

**Knowledge:** A structured understanding of reality recorded as text at a specific Scale Level. Knowledge does not equal reality — it is a lossy record of reality (see Section 10). For LLMs, Knowledge is the text in training data.

**Scale:** Simultaneously accommodating adequately sufficient knowledge systems across multiple distinct Scale Levels.

Scale is not data volume. The Knowledge at each Scale Level has its own combinatorial Logic, its own empirical parameters, its own emergent phenomena, and requires sufficient Knowledge volume to support effective operation. The larger the Scale, the more local knowledge systems must be maintained simultaneously. For LLMs, Scale is the diversity of training data — how many distinct Scale Levels it spans.

# Part II: The Core Argument — Combinatorial Manifestation Varies with Scale Level

---

## 4. Logic Is Cross-Scale Level. Rules Are Cross-Scale Level. Combinatorial Manifestation Is Not.

Logic holds across Scale Levels. The four fundamental forces are also cross-Scale Level — gravity, electromagnetism, the strong nuclear force, and the weak nuclear force all exist at every Scale Level. The rules have not changed; Logic has not changed.

But any concrete phenomenon in reality is not the expression of a single rule — it is the combinatorial effect of multiple rules at a specific Scale Level. Why is water liquid? Electromagnetism dominates intermolecular interactions; quantum mechanics determines electron orbitals; gravity is negligible at this Scale Level. On a neutron star, the same four forces are present, but the relative weights of the strong nuclear force and gravity are entirely different, and matter behaves entirely differently.

The rules have not changed, but the weights of the rules change with Scale Level. The mode of combination changes with Scale Level.

Truth is not rules. Truth is the Combinatorial Manifestation of rules at a specific Scale Level. This combinatorial mode cannot be logically derived from the rules themselves — it can only be observed from the reality of that Scale Level.

---

## 5. This Argument Does Not Depend on Emergence

Combinatorial Manifestation varies with Scale Level without requiring emergence to explain it. The field structures of electromagnetism and gravity are nearly identical (both follow the inverse square law), but electromagnetism is approximately 10³⁶ times stronger than gravity, and electric charges cancel each other while mass does not. These two simple differences alone are sufficient for the same rules to produce entirely different Combinatorial Manifestations at the atomic Scale Level versus the astronomical Scale Level.

No chaos, no nonlinearity, no complex systems theory is required. Combinatorial Manifestation varying with Scale Level is an observed fact more fundamental than emergence. Emergence is an aggravating condition — it makes differences in Combinatorial Manifestation more dramatic — but it is not a necessary condition. Even without emergence, the scale-dependence of rule weights alone is sufficient to establish the Information Trilemma.

This matters because emergence is itself a contested concept — some argue emergence can be reduced. The Information Trilemma does not need to fight that battle. Its premises rest only on observed facts that no one can deny.

---

## 6. Combinatorial Manifestation Varies Across Scale Levels — Why the Trilemma Holds

Combinatorial Manifestation differs across Scale Levels. This is not theory — it is observed fact. The Information Trilemma does not need to explain why it differs; it only needs to observe that it does. Scale Levels are defined by differences in Combinatorial Manifestation (see Section 3). The Information Trilemma does not explain why multiple distinct Scale Levels exist — just as the Second Law of Thermodynamics does not need to explain why entropy increases; it observes that entropy does increase, and derives constraints from that fact. This is a deliberate boundary drawn by this paper, not an omission.

Derivation path:

1. Truth at Scale Level₁ depends on the Combinatorial Manifestation of rules at Scale Level₁, denoted Combinatorial Manifestation(Scale Level₁).

2. Truth at Scale Level₂ depends on the Combinatorial Manifestation of rules at Scale Level₂, denoted Combinatorial Manifestation(Scale Level₂).

3. Combinatorial Manifestation(Scale Level₁) ≠ Combinatorial Manifestation(Scale Level₂). This is an observed fact.

4. A Logic framework L₁ established at Scale Level₁ is based on Combinatorial Manifestation(Scale Level₁). When applied to Scale Level₂, because Combinatorial Manifestation(Scale Level₂) ≠ Combinatorial Manifestation(Scale Level₁), it is not guaranteed to correspond with Truth at Scale Level₂.

5. When Scale simultaneously covers Scale Level₁ and Scale Level₂, unified Logic and Truth at all Scale Levels cannot be simultaneously satisfied.

For LLMs, this means: the training data simultaneously contains Knowledge from Scale Level₁, Scale Level₂, Scale Level₃ through Scale Levelₙ — each true within its own Scale Level, but mutually Logic-incompatible. The model uses a single set of parameters to fit all this data, and must at some point sacrifice either logical consistency or factual Truth.

---

## 7. Distinction from Unified Field Theory

The Information Trilemma is not saying rules cannot be unified — it is saying Combinatorial Manifestation cannot be captured by a unified Logic framework.

Even if someone found a unified field theory tomorrow — a set of equations beautifully unifying the four forces — to use those equations to calculate the boiling point of water, you would still need to substitute Scale Level-specific boundary conditions, make approximations, and choose truncations. The equations are unified, but the path from equations to concrete phenomena is different at every Scale Level. Unified field theory unifies rules, not Combinatorial Manifestation.

The constraint of the Information Trilemma is not at the rule layer — it is at the combinatorial layer between rules and phenomena. Even with unified rules, Combinatorial Manifestation still cannot cross Scale Levels. This means the trilemma's constraint is deeper than intuition suggests.

---

## 8. The Trilemma's Self-Reinforcement

The Knowledge within each Scale Level can be individually perfect in Logic. Planetary mechanics is internally consistent; quantum mechanics is internally consistent. But the moment you cross Scale Levels, logical consistency breaks — at a black hole's singularity, general relativity says density is infinite; quantum mechanics says this is meaningless. Both are correct; together they contradict.

The more complete and rigorous the Knowledge within each Scale Level, the sharper the Logic conflict when crossing Scale Levels — because each has developed a complete system, and the foundational assumptions of these systems do not hold at other Scale Levels.

The better each of the two vertices performs individually, the more thoroughly the third collapses.

For LLMs: the higher the quality of training data and the more complete the coverage within each domain, the more apparent the contradictions in cross-domain reasoning. Not because the data is bad — it is because good data is inherently in conflict with other good data.

# Part III: The Specific Toxicity of Training Data

---

## 9. Every Domain's Data Carries Its Own Poison

LLMs' training data comes from all domains. Every domain's data carries cross-Scale-Level contradictions — not a data quality problem, but the structure of reality itself.

Physics data contradicts itself across Scale Levels. General relativity and quantum mechanics are each internally consistent but mutually incompatible. The model has learned two frameworks, and no data tells it when to use which — because humans themselves have not yet figured this out.

Biology data describes a system that does not pursue logical consistency. The same functional need has entirely different solutions across species; within a single species, every layer from gene to individual produces emergent phenomena that the layer below cannot predict; and biological organisms are full of evolutionary redundancy and contradictions. Asking the model to learn logically consistent Knowledge from this data is asking the data to provide something it never had.

Humanistic data not only carries cross-Scale-Level contradictions but also carries "Truth" shaped by power. The same fundamental rules (humans need food; humans die) combine into entirely different institutions across different times and places, and the mode of combination largely comes from active construction by power. The model has no ability to distinguish between what is observed fact and what is a narrative constructed within a particular power structure.

On the surface these look like different problems in different domains. Underneath, they are the same structure. It is not that physics data carries a physics-specific poison and biology a biology-specific one — there is only one poison; each domain has simply been infected from a different angle. The difference lies in the dimensionality of the combinatorial variables: physical combinations arise naturally and have lower dimensionality; humanistic combinations are shaped by power and have extremely high dimensionality. The constraint strength of the Information Trilemma increases accordingly.

# Part IV: Aggravating Conditions — The Defects of Knowledge Itself

---

The variation of Combinatorial Manifestation with Scale Level is a sufficient condition for the Information Trilemma. Even with perfect, lossless Knowledge, as long as Combinatorial Manifestation differs across Scale Levels, the Information Trilemma holds.

But the Knowledge LLMs ingest is not perfect and lossless. The defects of Knowledge are observable facts that make the trilemma's constraints more severe.

---

## 10. Knowledge Is Lossy

Knowledge does not completely reflect reality. This is an observed fact.

The loss is structural: time is lost — dynamic processes become static records; the continuous transitions between Scale Levels are lost — becoming discrete, discipline-separated systems; the complete details of Combinatorial Manifestation are lost — becoming empirical parameters and magic numbers; context is lost — becoming seemingly universal propositions.

LLMs' training data is the textualization of this lossy Knowledge. Every layer discards something, but every layer's output looks complete.

---

## 11. The Specific Defects of Knowledge

**Experiments are not reproducible.** A large number of research results cannot be independently replicated. The replication crisis is not a problem specific to any single domain — it is a structural feature of the Knowledge-production process. Expecting to train a model on this data and obtain one that satisfies the Information Trilemma is not computation; it is wishful thinking.

**Empirical parameters cannot be explained.** Theories that actually work are often full of empirical parameters and magic numbers. An engineer dials in a working parameter; ask why it is 0.7 rather than 0.8, and the answer is "I tried it and it worked." Papers write "empirically determined" — translated into plain language: "I do not know why, but it works." Knowledge that is genuinely useful is precisely the Knowledge least conforming to the standard of "logically rigorous."

**Conclusions contradict each other.** Different studies reach opposite conclusions on the same question — not because someone made an error, but because different experimental conditions constitute different Scale Levels, and Truth at different Scale Levels can legitimately differ.

**Theories post-hoc rationalize.** A large portion of theoretical explanation is found after the fact, not derived beforehand. Why does this learning rate work in deep learning? Why set dropout at 0.5? Why is ReLU better than sigmoid? The answers are mostly post-hoc attribution, not a priori deduction.

Combinatorial Manifestation divergence is the structural constraint; Knowledge defects are compounding deterioration. Both act simultaneously on LLMs' training data.

# Part V: Specific Constraints on LLMs

---

## 12. LLMs Are a Re-Compression of Incomplete Knowledge

Knowledge already incompletely reflects reality. LLMs train on this incomplete Knowledge — recompressing an already-compressed foundation. Every layer discards something, but every layer's output looks complete and confident.

LLM training does not require "data that is true and logically rigorous" — it requires data that covers the diversity of human language and Knowledge. Scale here is not just "large volume" but "large diversity" — spanning a large number of distinct Scale Levels. To cover the breadth of human Knowledge, one must include data with conflicting viewpoints, imprecise Logic, and even factual errors, because that is what real-world Knowledge looks like.

---

## 13. The Structural Limitations of Algorithms

Current mainstream architectures (Transformers) are fundamentally performing statistical correlation — not logical reasoning. The "Logic" they learn is patterns induced from large amounts of text, not deductions from axioms. Statistical induction and logical deduction are two fundamentally different cognitive mechanisms; forcing one architecture to do both simultaneously necessarily involves compromise somewhere.

Modeling can partially circumvent this limitation — physical simulation, weather forecasting, protein folding: these models contain time, causality, and continuous evolution. But all modeling is Scale Level-bound. What modeling can do is recover causal structure within a closed system with known rules and a known Scale Level. What modeling cannot do is determine which model to use — when to apply Newtonian mechanics, and when to switch to relativity. That judgment is not something modeling can resolve. The success of modeling is not breaking through the Information Trilemma — it is abandoning the Scale vertex.

---

## 14. Hallucination Is Structural but Manageable

LLMs systematically favor Knowledge combinations that have large Scale, apparently rigorous Logic, but uncontrollable Truth.

Because the essence of genuinely useful Knowledge is often hidden in the author's feel, intuition, and failed experiments never written into papers — things that have never been turned into text. LLMs can only learn Knowledge that has already been recorded as text, and that Knowledge is already incomplete.

At the same time, non-reproducible experimental results, overturned conclusions, and mutually contradictory meta-analyses in the training data are indistinguishable from reliable Knowledge at the pure text level. LLMs have no ability to tell them apart, because distinction requires not text analysis but interaction with reality at a specific Scale Level — and LLMs have only text, not the world.

LLMs' hallucination problem is not a bug. It is the inevitable cost of using a unified architecture to span incommensurable Scale Levels. Natural language never carries Scale Level markers — when Knowledge is recorded as text, the lost context includes "at which Scale Level this proposition holds" (see Section 10). LLMs learn this text, and their output inherits the form of "seemingly universal." The Information Trilemma necessarily causes Truth to fail at certain Scale Levels (¬Truth), but since output carries no Scale Level qualifier, hallucination appears as confident error rather than marked uncertainty.

But "structural" does not mean "unimprovable." Hallucination rates have been steadily declining. The methods of reduction confirm the Information Trilemma exactly: the way humans evade the Information Trilemma is to divide into disciplines — constraining Scale; the way LLMs evade the Information Trilemma is to divide tasks and domains and add external verification — RAG temporarily narrows Scale to retrieved documents; tool-calling outsources Truth judgment to verifiable systems; domain fine-tuning locks a general model to a specific Scale Level. Different strategies, same Logic: all trade Scale for the compatibility of Logic and Truth.

The Information Trilemma is not saying hallucination cannot be reduced — it is saying every method of reducing hallucination sacrifices Scale. You cannot reduce hallucination to zero while keeping Scale fully open.

---

## 15. Data Cleaning Is a Two-out-of-Three Choice

Data cleaning is making trade-offs among the three vertices. This is the most direct engineering application of the Information Trilemma.

Tighten the cleaning rules, and logical consistency improves — but you cut out data that appears contradictory yet actually reflects Truth at different Scale Levels. Loosen the cleaning rules, and diversity is preserved — but noise and contradictions enter. No set of rules can simultaneously maximize all three vertices. What you are doing is a two-out-of-three choice.

Specific pain points:

On the same medical question, a 2010 meta-analysis and a 2024 conclusion reach opposite findings — which do you keep? Keep both: Logic contradicts. Keep only the newer: you may discard Truth that still holds under specific conditions — the 2010 conclusion may remain correct for a specific population or dosage. This cannot be resolved by better rules, because the definition of "better" is itself Scale Level-dependent.

On the same engineering parameter, the textbook says 0.7; a senior engineer's blog says 0.85 under certain conditions. Remove the blog and you lose practical Knowledge; keep it and you introduce a contradiction with the textbook. And what those "certain conditions" are is often not explicitly recorded — it is embedded in the engineer's feel, never turned into structured text.

Cross-cultural data is even more problematic. The same legal concept has different meanings in different jurisdictions; the same historical event is described differently in different countries' textbooks. Whose version do you privilege when cleaning? Choose one, and you sacrifice Truth at a specific Scale Level; keep both, and Logic becomes inconsistent.

The priority ordering among the three vertices is not fixed either. An engineer's magic number — usually beneath serious consideration — becomes a lifeline when other sources fail. Even if an information system can make trade-offs among the three vertices, it does not know which trade-off to prioritize at this moment. That judgment requires understanding the current context, and context is a Scale Level question — which circles back to the limitations of the Information Trilemma itself.

What data-cleaners should take away: you are not looking for an optimal solution — you are making a two-out-of-three choice. Be clear about which vertex your current task requires sacrificing, then sacrifice it consciously, rather than pretending you can have all three.

# Part VI: Boundary Conditions and Structured Description

---

## 16. Where the Trilemma Does Not Apply

Fractals are structures where the same rule produces the same Combinatorial Manifestation at all Scale Levels — the same pattern repeating infinitely. Fractals achieve cross-Scale-Level consistency precisely because they trade away all complexity for scale invariance.

Geometry holds across Scale Levels, is logically consistent, and is true within its assumed domain. Geometry can hold across Scale Levels because it does not handle force combinations — it only describes the structure of space, without involving how things in that space interact.

The applicability condition for the Information Trilemma is: Combinatorial Manifestation varies with Scale Level. The criterion is interaction — where there is interaction, there is combination; combination can change with Scale Level; the Information Trilemma applies. Pure structural, non-interactive formal systems (geometry, fractals, pure mathematics) can be consistent across Scale Levels. Once actual phenomena are involved — physical, biological, humanistic — the Information Trilemma is present.

For LLMs, this draws a line: models can be cross-Scale-Level consistent in pure formal reasoning (mathematics, Logic), but once empirical Knowledge is involved, the constraints of the Information Trilemma activate.

## 17. Structured Description

The following is not a formal proof — it is a structured statement of observed facts and the derivation path.

```
Definitions:
  Scale     = simultaneously covering multiple Scale Levels
              (Scale Level₁, Scale Level₂, ..., Scale Levelₘ),
              with a sufficient Knowledge subset Knowledge(Scale Levelⱼ)
              at each Scale Level
  Logic     = for all propositions P ∈ system: ¬(P ∧ ¬P)
  Truth(Scale Level) = the system's output corresponds with verifiable
                       reality at that specific Scale Level

Observed Facts:
  F1: The fundamental rule set exists invariantly across Scale Levels
  F2: Concrete phenomena = Combinatorial Manifestation of the rule set
      at a specific Scale Level, denoted Combinatorial Manifestation(Scale Level)
  F3: When Scale Level₁ ≠ Scale Level₂,
      Combinatorial Manifestation(Scale Level₁) ≠ Combinatorial Manifestation(Scale Level₂)
      (observed fact; does not depend on emergence)

Derivation:
  From F2: Truth(Scale Level) depends on Combinatorial Manifestation(Scale Level)
  From F3: Combinatorial Manifestation(Scale Level₁) ≠ Combinatorial Manifestation(Scale Level₂)
  ∴  A Logic framework L₁ established at Scale Level₁ is based on
       Combinatorial Manifestation(Scale Level₁)
     When L₁ is applied to Scale Level₂, because
       Combinatorial Manifestation(Scale Level₂) ≠ Combinatorial Manifestation(Scale Level₁),
       Truth(Scale Level₂) is not guaranteed
  ∴  When Scale is open, unified Logic and Truth across all Scale Levels
     cannot be simultaneously satisfied

  Note: "not guaranteed" here is an induction from observed facts,
  not a mathematical proof of impossibility.
  The trilemma is an empirical rule, not a theorem.

Boundary Condition:
  If for all Scale Level₁, Scale Level₂:
    Combinatorial Manifestation(Scale Level₁) = Combinatorial Manifestation(Scale Level₂)
    (Combinatorial Manifestation does not vary with Scale Level, e.g., fractals),
  then the trilemma does not apply.

Aggravating Condition (Knowledge defects):
  Knowledge(Scale Level) incompletely reflects Combinatorial Manifestation(Scale Level)
    (observed fact)
  ∴  Even within a single Scale Level, Logic and Truth are not guaranteed
     to be simultaneously satisfied
  Combinatorial Manifestation divergence + Knowledge defects act together;
    the trilemma's constraints are stronger.

LLM Constraints:
  Full training dataset = ∪ Knowledge(Scale Levelⱼ), j = 1, ..., m
  LLMs fit the full training dataset with unified parameters θ
  From the trilemma: no θ exists such that Truth at all Scale Levels
    holds under unified Logic
  Mitigation strategies (RAG, tool-calling, domain fine-tuning)
    = constraining Scale to obtain Logic ∧ Truth
```

## 18. Relation to Other Trilemmas

Trilemmas form a family. The CAP theorem says distributed systems must choose two of: consistency, availability, and partition tolerance. Mundell-Fleming says one must choose two of: free capital flow, fixed exchange rates, and independent monetary policy. This paper's Information Trilemma says one must choose two of: Scale, Logic, and Truth.

The structure is the same: three good things cannot all be had simultaneously; the system has an inherent trade-off ceiling; the issue is not resources but structure.

The differences lie in the source and hardness of the constraint. CAP has a formal proof; Mundell-Fleming has model support. This paper's Information Trilemma is based on observed facts — an empirical rule, not a theorem. It is broader than CAP and Mundell-Fleming (applicable to all empirical knowledge systems) but softer (lacking formal proof). This paper does not pursue formal proof. The value of empirical rules in engineering practice does not depend on formalization — engineers need a framework to guide trade-offs, not a theorem that can pass peer review.

---

## 19. Scope and Limitations

**Applicable:** Any knowledge system in which Combinatorial Manifestation varies with Scale Level. LLMs' training data naturally belongs to this category, spanning physics, biology, the humanities, and all other domains of empirical Knowledge.

**Not applicable:** Pure formal systems in which Combinatorial Manifestation does not vary with Scale Level — fractals, geometry, pure mathematics. LLMs' performance in these domains is not constrained by the Information Trilemma.

**Limitations:** The Information Trilemma does not predict where specifically failure will occur. It does not rank the priority of the three vertices — priority is Scale Level-dependent, varying with context and supply and demand. It does not provide solutions — it describes structural constraints, not problems that engineering methods can overcome. The Information Trilemma does not explain the nature of Scale Level, nor why Combinatorial Manifestation varies with Scale Level — it only observes that it does, and derives constraints from that observation. The Information Trilemma is itself Knowledge, and is itself incomplete.
