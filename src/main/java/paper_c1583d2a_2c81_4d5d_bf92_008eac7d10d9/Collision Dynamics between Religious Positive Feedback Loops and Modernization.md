## Abstract

The three major religions each follow distinct dynamical paths (Huang, 2026, *Religion as an Information Flow System*): Christianity's evolutionary path (evolution), Islam's lockdown path (steady state), Buddhism's self-organization path (self-organization). But once the paths have been identified, one question remains unresolved: how does each path respond when it encounters modernization—an external positive feedback loop?

This paper proposes a collision model. Religion and modernization are two positive feedback loops competing for the same pool of resources. The core effect of modernization is the consumption of family redundancy (R). R is a time resource embedded in relational structures—not individual leisure, but the structural resource that enables the conditions for intergenerational transmission of xin to exist. When R is sufficient, transmission conditions are intact, and the religious positive feedback loop runs normally; when R is exhausted, transmission conditions vanish, and the loop is cut off from supply.

The three paths respond to R consumption in entirely different ways. The evolutionary path co-evolves with modernization; each collision round produces new variants; variants degrade from exclusionary elimination to market competition; the endgame is indistinguishability from the market. The lockdown path refuses to let R be consumed, using institutional power to maintain family structure, but faces repeated tug-of-war: M's external force drains R, the lockdown path rebounds and blocks it; institutional power corrupts and contaminates the signal end, the next generation actively rejects, suppression blocks again—but with each round the R baseline is lower, the cost of blocking higher, until it can no longer hold. The self-organization path is not immune—R consumption blocks the gateway to practice, temples transform into service providers—but internal differentiation is significant: traditions with more lockdown elements mixed in (Tibetan Buddhism) have greater resilience; the purest self-organization paths (Japanese Buddhism) degrade most thoroughly.

This paper verifies the collision model with three cases: white American Protestants (the evolutionary path at its purest, with the cleanest data, using the Black church as a counterexample of R supply), Turkey (a century of tug-of-war between the lockdown path and M, with Iran as supplementary verification of double-round dynamics), and Japanese Buddhism (the complete degradation trajectory of the self-organization path from the danka system to funeral Buddhism). The core causal chain in all three cases—M consumes R → intergenerational transmission of F breaks—holds in every instance.

What R carries is not only the intergenerational transmission of religion, but all non-quantifiable intergenerational transmission and risk buffering. After R is exhausted, a society has two modes of death: slow death (intergenerational transmission breaks → shared values vanish → fertility collapses) and fast death (risk buffering drops to zero → any shock directly pierces through society). After collapse comes violent reckoning; survivors are forced to rebuild relational structures; R slowly accumulates; new religious positive feedback loops restart. But in the path competition after collapse, the lockdown path and paths with lockdown elements (Catholicism) have structural advantages. The endpoint of the spiral depends on the race between R accumulation and M restart—if the window is long enough, a new civilization grows; if the window is too short, the spiral loses its recovery phase.

This paper is an independent verification case for *Religion as an Information Flow System* and a mirror image of *East Asian Involution Dynamics*—that paper treated transcendence mechanisms as a black box and examined modernization; this paper treats modernization as a black box and examines religion.

**Keywords:** Semantic Flow Dynamics, positive feedback loop, family redundancy, evolutionary path, lockdown path, self-organization path, secularization, intergenerational transmission

## I. The Problem

*Religion as an Information Flow System* identified three dynamical paths. Christianity follows the evolutionary path (6bbcc745-22c0-46ea-8a0a-63bd15c94e32.EvolutionaryPath): open channels (40a22ff8-9d90-4e1e-82f6-1fd80917c139.Channel), no signal lock (6bbcc745-22c0-46ea-8a0a-63bd15c94e32.SignalLock) on signals (40a22ff8-9d90-4e1e-82f6-1fd80917c139.Signal), repeated schisms and collisions, evolving survivors. Islam follows the lockdown path (6bbcc745-22c0-46ea-8a0a-63bd15c94e32.LockdownPath): closed channels, extreme signal lock, suppressed variation, steady state. Buddhism follows the self-organization path (6bbcc745-22c0-46ea-8a0a-63bd15c94e32.SelfOrganizationPath): no central channel, no signal lock, pure market competition (6bbcc745-22c0-46ea-8a0a-63bd15c94e32.MarketCompetition), no heresy judgment (6bbcc745-22c0-46ea-8a0a-63bd15c94e32.HeresyJudgment).

After the paths were identified, the original paper left an unfinished direction in Chapter Four: "Dynamical predictions for civilizational collision—when religions or civilizations on different paths meet, what happens at the signal end and the semantic end respectively?" The original paper marked this direction as "independent verification"—phenomena not considered when the framework was built are the strongest tests of the framework's explanatory power.

The collision this paper addresses is not between religions. It is the collision all religions face together: modernization.

Modernization (d5c8a3f7-9e2b-4d1a-b6f4-e0a2c8d4f6b8.ModernizationCycle) is a positive feedback loop (40a22ff8-9d90-4e1e-82f6-1fd80917c139.PositiveFeedbackLoop) (Huang, 2026, *East Asian Involution Dynamics*): work → income → improved quality of life → higher expectations → more work. This loop is self-reinforcing, resource-absorbing, and irreversible. Religion is also a positive feedback loop: core signal → transmission of xin → functional convergence (40a22ff8-9d90-4e1e-82f6-1fd80917c139.FunctionalConvergence) → institutionalization (6bbcc745-22c0-46ea-8a0a-63bd15c94e32.Institutionalization) → death pressure (6bbcc745-22c0-46ea-8a0a-63bd15c94e32.DeathPressure) driving the loop → cycle. Two positive feedback loops competing for the same pool of resources.

The question of this paper: what does the positive feedback loop of modernization do to each of the three religious paths?

The significance of this question extends beyond religious studies. The endgame of R exhaustion is not the death of religion—it is the collapse of civilization. Religion and modernization are both plants growing from the soil of R; two plants competing for the same soil, until the soil is exhausted and both die.

**Scope limitation.** The populations covered by the three paths differ enormously—Afghanistan and Indonesia are both Muslim; the United States and South Africa are both Christian. The variation within a single path can be larger than the variation between paths. This paper does not attempt to explain all changes in all religions worldwide with a single model. This paper selects three cases, choosing for each path the population where the path runs in its purest form, with the most complete historical trajectory and the cleanest data: the evolutionary path selects white American Protestants; the lockdown path selects Turkey; the self-organization path selects Japanese Buddhism. The selection criterion is not "most representative" but "most capable of testing the mechanism." Other populations (South American Catholicism, Indonesian Muslims, Taiwanese Buddhism) are variations under deviation from pure conditions that can be used to test model boundaries, but are outside the scope of this paper.

## III. The Model

### 3.1 Resource Competition between Two Positive Feedback Loops

Religion is not a belief system; it is a positive feedback loop.

```
Religious positive feedback loop F:
  F₀: Core signal enters the individual (40a22ff8-9d90-4e1e-82f6-1fd80917c139.Individual)
  F₁: Xin (40a22ff8-9d90-4e1e-82f6-1fd80917c139.Xin) transforms the signal into semantics
  F₂: Semantics functionally converge at the group level (signal patterns converge;
       semantic flows (40a22ff8-9d90-4e1e-82f6-1fd80917c139.SemanticFlow) need not be identical)
  F₃: The products of convergence are institutionalized as signal containers
       (40a22ff8-9d90-4e1e-82f6-1fd80917c139.SignalContainer)
  F₄: Death pressure drives a new generation of individuals into F₀
  → Return to F₀
```

Modernization is also a positive feedback loop (see *East Asian Involution Dynamics*, §3.1). The two loops compete for the same pool of resources: time, money, manpower, attention.

The structural asymmetry of the competition: modernization's returns are quantifiable (income, lifespan, comfort); religion's returns are non-quantifiable (sense of meaning, sense of belonging, comfort in the face of death). Quantifiable returns structurally crush non-quantifiable returns in resource competition—the three hours you spend at church every Sunday versus three hours of overtime, the return on overtime is visible; the return on church you cannot articulate. It is not absent; it is that what cannot be articulated always loses to what can be articulated in competition.

This asymmetry derives directly from the structural ceiling of Semantic Flow Dynamics: the semantic end is non-quantifiable (40a22ff8-9d90-4e1e-82f6-1fd80917c139, §8). Non-quantifiability is not merely an analytical limitation but also religion's structural disadvantage in resource competition.

### 3.2 Family Redundancy (R)

The effect of modernization on religion, regardless of how M's internal mechanisms work, comes down to one thing on religion's side: families go from having redundancy to having none.

**Terminological clarification.** "Intergenerational transmission of xin" is shorthand. Xin is an individual's consciousness state; it cannot be directly copied or transported—the Law of Dissipation (40a22ff8-9d90-4e1e-82f6-1fd80917c139.DissipationLaw) prohibits semantic flow from being losslessly encoded as signals, and xin is even less capable of being losslessly transmitted. What is transmitted intergenerationally is not xin itself, but the conditions that allow the next generation's xin to form: immersion in a specific semantic environment, observation of demonstration, co-presence with holders of xin. R carries precisely these conditions. All subsequent uses of "transmission of xin" in this paper refer to this meaning.

**Definition.** Family redundancy (R) is the time and resources embedded in relational structures that are not directly occupied by the modernization loop. Isolated leisure is not R—time must be embedded in relational structures capable of carrying intergenerational transmission (family, community, church) to constitute R. A homeless person has unlimited non-market time, but R is zero, because he has no relational structure to convert that time into transmission conditions. Conversely, when a homeless person walks into a church and rebuilds relationships, his time begins to become R—the church's function here is not transmitting signals but providing structure.

R is not a number; it is a set:

```
R.mother    Mother's non-market time
            Traditional: full-time at home; primary transmitter of xin
            M's effect: enters the labor market; time drops to zero

R.child     Child's non-institutionalized time
            Traditional: immersed in the family; passively receives xin
            M's effect: daycare → school → cram school → internet;
                       all time institutionalized; receives only signals

R.elder     Elder's presence in the family
            Traditional: three-generational household; living signal container
                       + transmitter of xin
                       Grandmother tells stories, takes children to rituals,
                       demonstrates attitudes toward death
            M's effect: nuclear family → nursing home;
                       intergenerational transmission compressed from three
                       generations to one, then to zero

R.kin       Kin network
            Traditional: extended family, clan, church community
                       Transmission of xin relies not only on parents;
                       the entire network transmits
            M's effect: urbanization scatters the clan;
                       community functions replaced by the market

R.ritual    Family ritual time
            Traditional: Sunday, Sabbath, Ramadan, ancestor worship, festivals;
                       fixed rhythms that interrupt M's loop,
                       forcing the family into T's frequency
            M's effect: rituals yield to work and consumption;
                       Christmas becomes a shopping festival;
                       holidays shift from T's signal to M's signal

R.space     Family's physical space
            Traditional: the home is the transmission field for xin;
                       it has a shrine, a Bible, a prayer corner
            M's effect: housing shrinks, space becomes functionalized;
                       the living room becomes a reception field for
                       smartphone signals
```

**The father's position.** The father is not a transmitter of xin; he is the anchor of family structure. In traditional society the father was already the one going out to work; he does not directly transmit xin—the mother does. But his presence maintains the integrity of the family structure: with the father present, R's components have a carrier; when the father is absent, the structure itself loosens, and R's carrying capacity declines. The higher rate of faith attrition in single-parent families is not because a transmitter is missing, but because the structure is missing an anchor. Modernization's effect on the father is not extracting him (he was already outside working) but ensuring that the resources he provides no longer convert into family redundancy—they flow directly back into M's loop. Earning more but spending more; income increases but redundancy does not increase, or even decreases.

**The mother's position.** The mother is not merely one component of R; she is R's organizer. The other components—R.child, R.ritual, R.space—provide raw materials (time, space, rhythm); the mother structures these raw materials into transmission conditions. A child comes home from school with a few hours left blank—it is the mother who arranges them into dinner, bedtime stories, weekend activities, holiday rituals. School consumes the daytime; the mother converts the remaining time into R. When other components are consumed, the mother can still sustain transmission with whatever remains; when the mother is extracted, all remaining time loses its organizer, is immediately taken over by M's institutions or screens, and the whole thing collapses. This is why R.mother is last in the consumption sequence but most lethal in effect—the preceding consumptions reduce raw materials; the consumption of R.mother dismantles the person who organizes the raw materials.

The mother is simultaneously the primary provider of daily immersion. A child does not learn xin in church—he learns it at home, watching how the mother lives, how she prays, how she faces death. Church provides signals; the family transmits xin. Signals without xin are noise. Grandparents, elders in the church, even teachers can partially bear the provision of transmission conditions—the definitions of R.elder and R.kin themselves imply this—but the mother's dual function (organizer + immersion provider) makes her irreplaceable. Schools—including church schools—are M's secondary signal transmission mechanism: they can transmit knowledge and standardized value signals, but without relational structure, without immersion, without demonstration, they cannot substitute for R's function. Students in church schools who can recite the Apostles' Creed does not mean F is running.

Existing research supports this judgment. A cross-national study from the University of Münster (Gärtner, Hennig, Müller, 2025) confirms that the family is the primary site of religious socialization and the mother is the key agent driving transmission. A global study from Oxford University (*Sociology of Religion*, 2025) provides clear evidence that a cross-cultural association exists between female employment and secularization, and that this relationship has been consistently underestimated in the academic literature.

**The consumption sequence of R.** R's components do not disappear simultaneously. M's expansion advances along two dimensions: first destroying space (extracting people from their native environment), then filling time (institutionalizing remaining non-market time).

Spatial destruction comes first. Traditional society had no "going to work"—smallholder farmers, artisans, sole proprietors; the workplace was the living space. M's first step is extracting people from their native environment and sending them into factories, offices, cities. Leave the village → R.kin dies (kin networks physically scattered by distance). Leave the parents → R.elder dies (intergenerational co-presence vanishes).

Temporal destruction follows. M first consumes the easiest time—children's time. Schools are M's institutional conscription of children's time: hand the children to the state and the market; the state and the market convert them into labor. Prussia mandated compulsory education as early as 1763; women's mass entry into the labor market is a twentieth-century phenomenon. R.child is extracted before R.mother, not after. The legitimacy of schools precedes the legitimacy of "mothers should go to work"—no one opposes the signal "education is a good thing." Schools transmit knowledge and skills, not xin. After children are institutionalized during the day, the transmission window compresses to after school and weekends—but as long as the mother is still home, she organizes the remaining time into a transmission structure, and F can still run. Then M extracts the mother → R.mother dies (enters the labor market). The organizer vanishes; remaining time collapses into blankness, taken over by after-school care and screens. This step requires overturning the default of gender roles, so it comes last, but its lethality is greatest.

R.ritual is squeezed by both spatial and temporal forces—people are no longer together (space), and the time they have together is already filled (time). R.space disappears last, because the house is still there—only nothing inside it carries any transmission function anymore.

Complete sequence: R.kin → R.elder → R.child → R.ritual → R.mother → R.space.

This sequence is not accidental; it is determined by M's pressure logic: consume what has the least resistance first (turning farmers into workers requires only economic incentives), consume what has the most resistance last (turning mothers into workers requires an entire reversal of social norms).

### 3.3 The Zero-Redundancy State and Market Substitution

When R is consumed to zero, the family enters the zero-redundancy state. All soft functions—value transmission, emotional bonding, ritual maintenance, crisis buffering—are either cut or outsourced.

The recipient of outsourcing is the market. The market's positive feedback loop recognizes only one signal: profit. Daycare does not transmit values; it transmits safety and efficiency. Schools do not transmit xin; they transmit knowledge and skills. Cram schools do not transmit meaning; they transmit competitiveness.

Every family function outsourced to the market has its semantic end stripped away, leaving only the signal end. Because semantics are non-quantifiable, the market will not pay for what is non-quantifiable, nor will it charge for it.

The complete mechanism of secularization is therefore three steps:

```
Step one: M first extracts children (schools), then extracts the mother (labor market)
          → The family enters the zero-redundancy state
Step two: Family functions outsourced to the market → the market transmits
          signals, not xin
Step three: An entire generation grows up in a pure-signal environment
          → Receives knowledge, skills, security; does not receive xin
          → M's signals have already shaped the direction of semantic flow,
            establishing resistance structures (40a22ff8-9d90-4e1e-82f6-1fd80917c139.Filtering.Resistance)
            (40a22ff8-9d90-4e1e-82f6-1fd80917c139.EpistemicBarrier)
          → Upon contact with religious signals, filtering
            (40a22ff8-9d90-4e1e-82f6-1fd80917c139.Filtering) blocks them directly;
            signals become noise (40a22ff8-9d90-4e1e-82f6-1fd80917c139.Noise)
          → It is not merely "no xin to transform
            (40a22ff8-9d90-4e1e-82f6-1fd80917c139.Transformation)";
            M's epistemic barrier actively repels religious signals
```

**R carries more than religion.** The intergenerational transmission that R carries is not only the conditions for xin formation. Values, trust, sense of obligation, attitudes toward death, the capacity for long-term thinking—all non-quantifiable intergenerational transmissions travel through the same channel. When R drops to zero, the channel breaks, and everything on the channel dies simultaneously—it is not that religion dies first and the rest follows; everything dies together. The death of religion is visible, because it has institutionalized indicators: attendance rates, self-identification, number of churches. The death of values is invisible; the death of obligation is invisible; the disappearance of long-term thinking is invisible—you cannot even discuss it, because there are no indicators. This paper tracks religion not because religion is more important than other things, but because it is the only observable one. A society with R at zero has two modes of death:

Slow death: intergenerational transmission breaks → shared values vanish → institutions run idle (signal end normal, semantic end at zero) → fertility collapses (the structural conditions for raising the next generation vanish; all costs of childbearing are priced by M; all returns are non-quantifiable; the result of rational calculation is not to reproduce) → population cannot sustain institutions → collapse.

Fast death: R at zero means all risk buffering has been outsourced to M's institutions—insurance, hospitals, nursing homes. In traditional society, when you were sick, your family took care of you; when you were old, your children supported you. M outsourced these functions to the market; when M runs normally, no problem. But M is a positive feedback loop, and the defining feature of a positive feedback loop is: when running, it absorbs everything; when it collapses, it drops to zero overnight. A society without R buffering is exposed to M's shocks—the lethality of a shock depends on its scale. Japan's kodokushi (solitary deaths) is the terminal image of R at zero—a person dies in an apartment and goes undiscovered for weeks, because no relational structure is monitoring whether he is still alive.

Both modes of death are running simultaneously.

**Delay effect.** Changes in R manifest in F with a one-generation delay. R↓ at generation t → F weakens at generation t+1 → F may break at generation t+2.

**Post-WWII acceleration.** World War II was the turbocharger of the modernization positive feedback loop. During the war, the state directly extracted mothers from families and sent them into factories. After the war, the welfare state, consumerism, and women's rights movements kept them in the labor market. In the span of a single generation, the nursery was dismantled. A brief religious revival appeared in Europe in the late 1940s (the afterwave of death pressure); comprehensive decline began in the 1960s (the delayed effect of family disintegration). Death pressure is immediate; family disintegration has a one-generation delay. The two forces point in opposite directions with different time scales; superimposed, the result is first a rise, then a fall.

### 3.4 Path Differentiation: How the Three Paths Respond to R Consumption

**The evolutionary path: co-evolution → degradation → disappearance.**

The core feature of the evolutionary path is open channels plus unlocked signals; variation is constantly generated. Facing R consumption, the evolutionary path's response is to produce new variants adapted to low-R environments. With each collision round, the new variants move closer to the market than the last.

The nature of this "moving closer" requires re-understanding. The evolutionary path was originally not market-oriented—it was exclusionary elimination. Heresy judgment, excommunication, the Inquisition—all were institutionalizations of zero-sum games: your doctrine is wrong; you must be destroyed. There is no heresy judgment in the market; in the market there is only whether you buy or not.

The evolutionary path's "adaptation" under R consumption pressure is essentially a degradation from exclusionary elimination to market competition—abandoning the zero-sum game and beginning to serve customers. Megachurches providing childcare, social events, entertainment, and financial seminars are supplementing functions outsourced by the family, but using market logic. Prosperity theology directly says "believing in God will make you rich"—T.direction flips from ≠M to =M.

Once the evolutionary path degrades to the market stage, it faces the native market species of the self-organization path. Meditation, mindfulness, yoga, "spiritual but not religious"—all are marketized derivatives of the Buddhist self-organization path. The evolutionary path entered the market halfway through; the self-organization path has been in the market from the start (pure market competition, no heresy judgment, skillful means = product diversification). The evolutionary path cannot outcompete the self-organization path's native species in the market, because it still carries doctrinal baggage—no matter how marketized prosperity theology gets, it still has to say Jesus Christ; a meditation app does not need to say anyone's name.

One layer deeper: the degradation of the evolutionary path does not eliminate demand. The human need for transcendent experience does not vanish because the evolutionary path degrades—death pressure is still there; the gap in meaning is still there. After the evolutionary path degrades, this demand flows toward substitute products on the market. The self-organization path's derivatives (meditation, mindfulness, yoga) carry no doctrinal baggage; the evolutionary path's degradation products (megachurches, prosperity theology) still carry the cross. What carries no baggage wins in the market over what does.

The endgame of the evolutionary path: adaptation until indistinguishable from the market. It is not killed; it adapts until it disappears. What dies is religiosity, not the label. The label is the last thing to fall off—people still check "Christian," but that check does not mean F is still running; it means the signal end has not fully detached yet. The dynamics inside have long been those of the market.

**Catholicism's special position.** Catholicism's signal end appears unified (the Pope, the sacramental system), but its semantic end long ago fractured. South American Catholicism has absorbed massive indigenous elements (the Virgin of Guadalupe, Día de los Muertos); European Catholicism is institutionalized and hierarchically rigid. Signal unity masks semantic fragmentation. Catholicism's ability to expand globally is precisely because it allows semantic-end localization as long as the signal end remains untouched. But this also means: South American Catholicism's semantics are already half-internalized, giving it greater resistance to R consumption than European Catholicism. European Catholicism remains in an externally institutionalized form; when R is taken, it collapses. Where Catholicism is still thriving—South America, the Philippines, Africa—the commonality is not that the Church is strong, but that the mother is still in the home.

**The lockdown path: refusal → confrontation → repeated tug-of-war → cannot hold.**

The lockdown path's signal end cannot change. The Quran is the final revelation; the channel is permanently closed. Facing R consumption, the lockdown path cannot produce new variants to adapt the way the evolutionary path can—because any new variant implies modification of the signal end, and the signal end cannot be modified.

The lockdown path's structural response is therefore: do not let M in.

Islam's strict norms on family structure—gender roles, marriage institutions, intergenerational authority—from a dynamical perspective, are the lockdown path protecting its own intergenerational transmission mechanism. It is not protecting the mosque; it is protecting the family. Let M in, and the family loosens; the family loosens, and R is consumed; R is consumed, and the transmission of xin breaks; xin breaks, and the locked signal becomes noise.

The lockdown path's means of protecting R is institutionalization—using religious authority to maintain family structure, restrict women's entry into the labor market, and uphold intergenerational authority. But institutionalization itself introduces a risk unique to the lockdown path: once the institution holds the power needed to protect R, that power can be corrupted, can be politically hijacked. The signal end shifts from "God's revelation" to "the regime's instrument"; the semantic end is contaminated.

Therefore, the lockdown path's encounter with M's pressure is not a march toward an endpoint, but a repeated tug-of-war.

```
External pressure: M forcibly drains R → Lockdown path rebounds
  (revolution, coup, table-flip)
  → Re-locks; R temporarily preserved
  → But M has not disappeared; urbanization, education, the internet
    continue draining R in the background
  → R baseline is lower than the previous round

Internal corrosion: Institutional power protecting R corrupts →
  Signal end is politically hijacked
  → The next generation actively rejects the entire package
  → The institution uses suppression to block rejection
  → But with each blocking, the signal end is more deeply contaminated
  → More people reject in the next round
```

The two lines can run simultaneously or alternately. Iran is the complete case: the Shah pursued external pressure → the Islamic Revolution flipped the table and blocked → the revolutionary regime pursued internal corrosion → the younger generation rejected → suppression blocked → but with each round the R baseline is lower. Turkey as well: Atatürk's forced secularization → religious rebound → military coups push back → AKP takes power and re-locks → internal corrosion begins to run → the younger generation secularizes. With each round of tug-of-war, the lockdown path operates at a lower R baseline.

The key is the ratchet: the lockdown path blocks each time, but what it blocks is M's offensive; it cannot block M's continuous background consumption of R. Revolution and table-flipping do not reverse urbanization; suppression does not reverse the internet. With each round, less R is in hand, the cost of blocking is higher, until it can no longer hold.

The mode of death for the lockdown path is fundamentally different from the evolutionary path's. The evolutionary path is slow dilution—diluted until you cannot recognize it; the label remains but religiosity is gone. The lockdown path is digital—all-in or all-out; there is no middle ground. This derives directly from the structure of an unchangeable signal end: you cannot "half-believe the Quran," so you can only fully believe or fully not believe. Once rejected, it is a clean break.

The endgame of the lockdown path facing M: repeated tug-of-war, each round at a lower R baseline, until it can no longer hold. It is not a one-time death but chronic bleeding. Yet the mode of bleeding is digital—each round loses not half a believer but a whole one.

**The self-organization path: commercialization → stratification → cost transfer → physical extinction.**

The self-organization path is not immune. Earlier analysis described the self-organization path as "not on the same track"—semantics have already entered the lifestyle; R consumption cannot dismantle what has already been internalized. This description is incomplete.

R consumption's effect on the self-organization path is not transmission rupture but structural deformation. The self-organization path's practice—meditation, precept-keeping, contemplation—requires vast amounts of time, and time is R. After R is absorbed by M, only two types of people can still follow the self-organization path: monastics (who have institutionally exited M's loop) and the wealthy with leisure (whose R is sufficient to sustain both M and T simultaneously). Ordinary believers have no R left; they can only consume services, not truly practice.

Buddhism therefore splits into a producer class (the sangha) and a consumer class (lay believers), connected by market transactions. Temples become service providers—lighting lamps, dharma assemblies, blessing ceremonies, memorial services. Believers pay; temples provide rituals; the transaction is complete. The semantic end is compressed into signal-end commodities.

Skillful means (6bbcc745-22c0-46ea-8a0a-63bd15c94e32.SkillfulMeans) originally meant "different methods for different capacities"; now it means "different products for different spending levels."

Cost transfer is the most concealed deformation. The self-organization path originally requires each person to walk it themselves—you practice yourself; you realize yourself. R is insufficient; you cannot walk it. So the cost is transferred to believers: you do not have time to practice—no matter; you donate money, and I practice on your behalf. Merit transference, memorial services for the dead, disaster-averting prayers—essentially purchasing others' practice outcomes with money. This is doctrinally untenable (realization is an individual event; it cannot be substituted), but in the market it works perfectly.

The self-organization path's response to R consumption is more concealed than the evolutionary path's degradation. Evolutionary degradation is visible—prosperity theology openly says "believe in God and get rich." The self-organization path's commercialization wears traditional robes—the temple is still a temple, the rituals are still rituals, but the dynamics inside have already shifted from individual practice to market transactions.

But commercialization is only the first stage. When R consumption deepens further—not merely family redundancy at zero, but fertility rates collapsing—the self-organization path moves from dynamical rewriting to physical extinction. Temples close not because Buddhism "lost," but because there are no consumers left.

The endgame of the self-organization path: practice becomes a class privilege; popular faith becomes service consumption; when consumers vanish, the forms themselves dissolve.

**Internal differentiation within the self-organization path.** The self-organization path is not monolithic. Tibetan Buddhism has mixed in channel designation and institutionalized intergenerational transmission mechanisms (tulku reincarnation, empowerment lineages, the guru as a necessary condition for practice); its resistance to R consumption is far higher than the pure self-organization path. Tibetan society's monastic tradition is endogenous, embedded in family structure (sending one son to become a monk is a family obligation), and does not depend on external administrative coercion. Japanese Buddhism's danka system was a purely administrative binding (shogunate decree), with no channel control—administrative binding breaks when rescinded; endogenous embedding breaks only when the family structure itself is dismantled. Japanese Buddhism is the purest case of the self-organization path, and therefore degrades the most thoroughly. The self-organization path's resilience correlates positively with how many lockdown elements it has mixed in.

**Cost differentiation of marketized products.** The fate of the self-organization path's products on the market depends on their cost structure. Low-cost products that solve describable problems (meditation, mindfulness—"I'm anxious," "I need focus") sell well but are easily replaced by pure market species—a meditation app is cheaper and more convenient than a temple retreat. High-cost products that solve indescribable problems (empowerment, lineage transmission—you must find a guru, build trust, follow long-term) do not sell easily but are irreplaceable—because all value is on the semantic end, and the semantic end cannot be marketized. Medium-cost products solving problems with secular alternatives (funeral Buddhism) are in the weakest position—the semantic end is already hollow, the signal end has no moat, and the secular funeral industry competes directly with more transparent pricing. The self-organization path's resilience therefore also depends on where its core product sits on this cost spectrum.

### 3.5 The Collapse-Restart Cycle

All three paths are inside the cycle.

```
R sufficient → Three paths each operate normally
  ↓
M consumes R → Evolutionary degrades, lockdown resists or self-destructs,
                self-organization commercializes
  ↓
R exhausted → Social collapse (slow death + fast death) → M itself fractures
  ↓
Violent reckoning (war, famine, population crash)
  ↓
Survivors forced to rebuild relational structures → R slowly accumulates
  ↓
Death pressure rises + R in place → F restarts
  (borrowing old signal containers)
  ↓
But the restart environment is a low-R environment
  → New F is natively adapted to the market
  → New F ≠ old F (signal end recognizable;
    dynamics unrecognizable)
  ↓
R re-accumulates after M collapses (M is no longer absorbing)
  → New T grows from new R
  → A new civilizational cycle begins
```

**The structural limitation of Great Awakenings.** A Great Awakening restarts signals at the church end but does not rebuild transmission at the family end. The signal end can be restarted in a single revival meeting—thousands weep and re-commit—but R does not recover in a single revival meeting. Family structure has not changed; children are still growing up on the internet; a generation's revival cannot survive two generations.

**The spiral is not a cycle.** With each turn, the R baseline drops a little. The family structure rebuilt after the first collapse is a little looser than before the collapse. The second turn, looser still. Each restart operates at a lower R baseline, producing religions more adapted to low-R environments—more individualized, less dependent on family, more marketized.

**The irreversibility of the spiral (ratchet effect).** The spiral can only go down, never up. What the ratchet locks is M's share—it only increases, never decreases. The time and energy M has consumed will not be returned: once you have used a flush toilet, you do not go back to a pit latrine; once you are accustomed to dual income, you do not go back to single income. M's positive feedback loop has encoded "declining living standards" as the greatest fear; the baseline of material expectations is permanently raised. R is what M leaves behind. M's share continuously expands; R continuously shrinks; whether R is embedded in family or church does not change this direction. R's recovery requires M to return time and energy; the ratchet does not allow it; therefore R recovers only after M collapses—not because you choose to go back, but because there is no choice. War, plague, imperial collapse. After Rome's collapse, Europe's population dropped by more than half from its peak; cities dissolved; the manorial economy returned; mothers went back home—not because Christianity persuaded them, but because the option of "going to work" no longer existed. M collapses, the ratchet loosens, R automatically recovers, T grows from the new R.

There is no historical case of "actively restoring R while M is still running." Korean Christianity's explosive growth is not a counterexample—it occurred after the Korean War, before economic takeoff, when death pressure was extremely high and R had not yet been drained. After economic takeoff, R was drained, and Korean Christianity is now also declining. It is not the spiral going upward; it is a normal pulse within the spiral—death pressure triggers F's restart, then M continues to consume R, and F continues to decline.

**The scale of collapse.** "Collapse" in this paper does not refer to economic shocks of any magnitude. Shocks have three scales; only the third triggers the collapse-restart cycle:

Individual unemployment—M temporarily releases one person's time, but M's infrastructure (schools, markets, urban structure) is still running; the ratchet has not loosened at the societal level. This person has time but no relational structure around to embed it in; R does not recover.

Economic crisis—M partially shuts down; a cohort is thrown out; but institutions remain; M will restart. A financial crisis pierces individual risk buffering, not civilization's infrastructure. Japan's lost three decades—M slowed but did not collapse; R did not recover; temples continued to vanish.

Civilizational collapse—M's infrastructure is physically destroyed: schools are gone, markets are gone, urban order is gone. The ratchet loosens not because people want to go back, but because there is no choice—no factory to go to work in, no school to send children to, no market to outsource family functions to. Only at this scale does R truly recover. Rome's collapse was at this scale.

The collapse-restart cycle in this paper refers to the third kind. The first two scales of shock accelerate slow death (piercing individuals, exposing the vulnerability of R at zero), but do not trigger restart, because M is still present and the ratchet has not loosened.

**The endpoint of the spiral.** The spiral does not descend infinitely, because the endpoint of R exhaustion is not religious individualization—it is civilization itself becoming unsustainable. After M collapses, R re-accumulates, because there is no M to absorb it. New T grows from the new R, and a new civilizational cycle begins.

**Collapse is not a peaceful transition.** Between M's collapse and R's rebuilding there is no seamless handoff—there is violent reckoning. M's collapse means all functions provided by the market are cut off overnight—security, food distribution, medical care, order. R is already at zero; family structure is also gone; there is no buffer. The result is violent competition for resources: war, famine, plague, population crash. After the collapse of the Roman Empire, Europe's population dropped more than half from its peak. After violent reckoning, survivors are forced to re-enter small-scale relational structures—because without the market, if you do not cooperate with others you die. Families, villages, tribes re-form—not voluntarily, but under survival pressure. R at this stage is coerced into existence, measured in generations to centuries. Only when R accumulates to a sufficient level, and death pressure is extremely high (the afterwave of violent reckoning), do conditions exist for a new F to start.

The Roman Empire is the complete case of this cycle. Rome's state religion (Christianity after Constantine) was an appendage of M—T.direction = M; the Church was the legitimacy supplier for the empire; episcopal appointments were tied to political power; church expansion was tied to military expansion. The signal end was Christian; the dynamics were M's. This was the early version of the lockdown path's internal corrosion—the signal end politically hijacked. M collapsed; the state religion died with it. But the signal containers remained (scripture, creeds, remnant believers). What survived was not the state religion but the monasteries. Benedictine monasteries did not depend on imperial order—they were the order. They rebuilt R in the ruins: fixed daily rhythms (R.ritual—seven daily prayers), communal living structure (R.kin), intergenerational inheritance (adopting orphans, training apprentices), physical space (the monastery itself), knowledge preservation (copying scriptures). Monasteries were the R suppliers after collapse, identical in logic to the Black church. The medieval Catholicism that grew from monasteries shared a recognizable signal end with the state religion (same Bible, same creeds), but its dynamics were entirely different—T.direction flipped from =M to ≠M. The Investiture Controversy between Pope and Emperor was essentially the maintenance of T ≠ M. This is why Catholicism could endure for a thousand years.

**Path competition after collapse.** But what grows after collapse is not necessarily the Christian model. All three major religions' signal containers are sufficiently durable—scripture does not disappear because civilization collapses; the Quran is memorized in human minds; the Bible has been printed in billions of copies; Buddhist sutras are stored in temples. Collapse destroys F, not T. F's restart will borrow old containers, but new F is not old F. The amplitude of variation correlates with the path—the evolutionary path varies most per round (the signal end permits rewriting), the lockdown path varies least per round (the signal end cannot be changed; variation is squeezed to the interpretive end), the self-organization path falls in between (signal containers are inherently numerous and soft).

The post-collapse environment—low R, high death pressure, no order—is precisely the lockdown path's home turf. The evolutionary path is already marketized; without the market, it has nothing. The self-organization path has no organizational mobilization capacity. Only the lockdown path can immediately provide order in chaos (signal end unchanged, rules clear) and actively rebuild R (mandatory family structure norms, gender roles, intergenerational authority). Islam has historically expanded fastest in the ruins of imperial collapse—the Persian Empire's collapse, the disintegration of Roman order in North Africa, the nomadic zones of Central Asia.

Catholicism also has structural advantages after collapse. It is the species within the evolutionary path closest to the lockdown path—the signal end has unified authority (the Pope), non-negotiable core doctrines (the sacramental system), and explicit family norms (anti-abortion, anti-contraception, anti-divorce). At the same time, the semantic end allows localization, enabling it to grow in the ruins of different cultures. The adaptability of the evolutionary path plus the resilience of the lockdown path—occupying both ends.

The directional prediction the framework can produce: with each collapse-rebuild cycle, the structural advantage of the lockdown path and paths with lockdown elements (Catholicism) increases, because the evolutionary path becomes more marketized with each round, the self-organization path becomes more commercialized with each round, and only lockdown structure remains unchanged. But who ultimately prevails depends on the specific conditions of the arena—geography, population, military—which are outside the framework's jurisdiction.

The full cycle:

```
T builds civilization → M grows from within civilization → M absorbs R
→ F deforms → R exhausted → T fails → M collapses
→ R re-accumulates → New F grows → New T → New civilization → ...
```

**The limitation of causal language.** The arrows describe the sequence of dominance transfer within a dynamical trajectory, not a linear causal chain. In a positive feedback loop there is no independent variable—T and civilization mutually constitute each other; M and R mutually define each other within a zero-sum constraint. "M consumes R" is shorthand; the precise formulation is: M and R compete for the same resource pool; when M's share expands, R's share is constrained to shrink. "R consumption dismantles F" is also shorthand; the precise formulation is: the operating conditions for F (R) are no longer met. This paper uses causal syntax (subject + verb + object) in the main text for readability; the underlying logic is always a simultaneous constraint relationship, not one end pushing the other.

R is the soil; T and M are both plants growing from the soil. Two plants competing for the same soil, until the soil is exhausted, both die, then the soil slowly recovers, and new plants grow again.

## IV. Empirical Comparison

### 4.1 The Evolutionary Path: White American Protestants

**Why this population.** White American Protestants are the purest case of the evolutionary path. All four Great Awakenings ran to completion within this population. The consumption of R has a precise timeline. The GSS has tracked since 1972, Pew since 2007, PRRI since 2006; the data span is sufficient to observe the delay effect. South African Christianity is contaminated by colonial and racial variables; South American Catholicism is contaminated by localization and internalization elements; neither is clean enough.

**The timeline of R consumption.** The U.S. female labor force participation rate was 33.9% in 1950, rose to 43.3% in 1970, and peaked at 60% in 1999. The participation rate for women aged 25–54 was even more dramatic: from 36.8% in 1950 to 76.8% in 1999. The consumption of R.mother was most intense during the 1960s–80s.

**Verification of the delay effect.** The model predicts that changes in R manifest in F with a one-generation delay (15–25 years). The large-scale consumption of R.mother occurred in the 1960s–80s; the accelerated decline of white Protestants began in the 1990s: white evangelicals fell from 23% in 2006 to 14% in 2020; white mainline Protestants fell from 18% to 13%. The share of American adults identifying as Christian dropped from 78% in 2007 to 71% in 2014, then to 62% in 2023–24. The time gap is approximately one generation, consistent with the model's prediction.

**The trajectory of evolutionary degradation.** The four Great Awakenings corroborate the spiral of each restart at a lower R baseline: the First Great Awakening (1730s–40s) → emotional revival within the church framework. The Second (1790s–1840s) → new denominations (Methodism, Mormonism). The Third (1850s–1900s) → the Social Gospel movement. The Fourth (1960s onward) → megachurches, prosperity theology. Each round produces a version more marketized than the last. Prosperity theology is the concrete instance of the evolutionary path degrading into the market: T.direction = M—"believe in God and you will get rich." Megachurches provide financial seminars, social events, entertainment programs. The signal end still carries the cross; the semantic end is already market logic.

**Explaining the "bottoming-out stabilization."** Pew 2023–24 data shows the Christian share stabilizing after 2019, fluctuating between 60–64%. This is not a counterexample of the downward spiral; it is precisely the signal that degradation is complete. What stabilizes is the signal-end self-identification label, not F's intergenerational transmission. What dies is religiosity, not the label. The label is the last thing to fall off. 62% of people still check "Christian," but that check does not mean F is still running—the dynamics inside have long been those of the market. The number stops there because degradation is already complete: it is no longer religion, so it cannot "continue declining." What declined was religion; what stabilizes is the cultural relic.

**Harvesting the spiritual market.** The "spiritual but not religious" population in America continues to grow. Their spiritual consumption points almost entirely toward derivatives of the self-organization path—meditation, mindfulness, yoga, cosmic energy. The human need for transcendent experience does not vanish because the evolutionary path degrades. After the evolutionary path degrades, demand flows to baggage-free substitute products on the market—the self-organization path's native species.

**The Black church counterexample.** Black American Christianity's resistance to secularization is far higher than that of white Protestants. The difference is not in doctrine but in R's source. White Protestants' R is supplied by the family; the church provides only signals—when M drains the family's R, the church dies with it. The Black church itself is the supplier of R. Slavery destroyed all of Africa's R (kin networks, intergenerational structures, rituals, language); the Black church rebuilt R in a zero-R environment: community center (R.kin), intergenerational connection venue (R.elder), weekly fixed rhythms (R.ritual), physical space (R.space), an environment where children are immersed (R.child). Black people go to church not because they have leisure, but because the church itself is the structure of R. This refines the model: it is not "American Christianity is declining" but "Christianity whose R is supplied by the family is declining; Christianity whose R is supplied by the church itself is still alive." The source of R need not be the family—any institution that provides relational structure can serve as a supplier of R.

**People still go to church at the deepest point of modernization.** This is not a counterexample of the model. Even in the most modernized places, death pressure remains—M extends lifespan but does not cancel death. Those who still attend church correspond to three distinct mechanisms: First, death pressure triggers—a cancer diagnosis, bereavement, a midlife crisis directly opens F₀; the individual actively seeks signals; but without R to support it, a generation's revival cannot survive two generations. Second, the church provides R—people go to church because they are lonely; the church provides community and belonging; the driving force is not F₀ but the need for R; this is the consumer behavior of "label still on, religiosity already gone." Third, residual R—certain families' R has not yet reached zero (the grandmother still takes the grandchildren; Sundays still go to church); F is still running; but the model predicts they are on a countdown. The trend is that the second type replaces the third—the church shifts from being F's carrier to being R's supplier; religiosity vanishes; community function remains.

### 4.2 The Lockdown Path: Turkey

**Why this population.** Turkey is the case where the lockdown path's tug-of-war with M has lasted the longest and produced the cleanest data. Since Atatürk's forced secularization in 1923, the lockdown path and M have been pulling against each other in the same population for a century, and the struggle is still unfinished. Every round of tug-of-war has clear historical records: Atatürk's forced secularization (external pressure) → religious rebound → military coups push back (external pressure again) → AKP takes power and re-locks (blocks again) → internal corrosion begins to run (younger generation secularizes). Indonesian Muslims never followed a pure lockdown; the variables are too dirty. Turkey provides the complete tug-of-war process, and its testing power for the model is strongest.

**Iran's supplementary value.** Iran runs the same tug-of-war but with more distinct rounds: the Shah's forced modernization (external pressure) → Islamic Revolution flips the table and blocks (re-locks) → revolutionary regime's institutional corruption (internal corrosion) → younger generation's mass secularization → 2022 hijab protests → suppression blocks again. Iran is currently running the second round of "what happens after blocking"—with each round, less R in hand, higher cost of blocking.

**R consumption and the lockdown tug-of-war.** Turkey's female labor force participation rate shows an anomalous trajectory: from approximately 30% in the 1990s, it first dropped to a low of 23% in 2005, then rose back to 36% in 2024. This first-drop-then-rise trajectory directly reflects the lockdown path's operation: urbanization extracted rural women from the fields (R.kin dies first), but the lockdown path kept them at home in the urban environment (R.mother temporarily preserved); thus the female participation rate actually dropped first—not because M was retreating, but because the lockdown path activated its protection of R.mother in the urban environment. But M continued to apply pressure; after 2005 the R.mother defense line began to be breached; the female participation rate rose back.

**Verification of external pressure and rebound.** Atatürk's forced secularization and Iran's Shah case both verify the first stage of the tug-of-war: M forcibly drains R through state power → the lockdown path rebounds. Turkey's military carried out multiple coups to maintain the secular system; each was a case of M attempting to force its way in—each time the lockdown path blocked, but each time it blocked at a lower R baseline.

**Verification of internal corrosion.** This is the Turkey case's most important contribution to the model. KONDA's 2024 survey shows that self-identified "devout" Turks dropped from 55% in 2008 to 46% in 2025; the non-religious/atheist share rose from 2% to 8%. More crucially, this decline occurred against the backdrop of twenty years of AKP governance actively promoting religiosity.

Turkey's Ministry of Education itself acknowledged that "young people are sliding toward deism." Within imam hatip religious schools—schools specifically training religious personnel—students were turning to deism and atheism. An Islamic professor reported that within a single year, 17 female students who still wore headscarves privately told him they had left Islam and considered themselves atheists. Gezici Research Center's 2020 survey found that 28.5% of Turkey's Generation Z identified as non-religious.

Academic research confirms that "despite the AKP's prioritization of religious education, expansion of the Directorate of Religious Affairs' budget and influence, and increased visibility of Islamic non-state actors, Turkish youth have become more secular, less devout, and more liberal on marriage and women's issues." Observers noted that the politicization and state institutionalization of religion itself drives people toward secular identity—"government control over religion and state provision typically alienates people from religious belief."

This is not the slow dilution of the evolutionary path. This is the lockdown path's digital rupture: the signal end cannot change, so you cannot "half-believe"; you can only fully accept or fully reject. After the institution turns the signal end into a political tool, what the young reject is not "this version of Islam" (that would be the evolutionary path's response—switch to another version) but the entire package. A clean break.

**The relationship between digital rupture and population-level data.** Population-level data shows gradual change—the devout dropped from 55% to 46% over 17 years. This is not a counterexample of the lockdown path. The "digital" descriptor of the lockdown path describes the individual's decision structure: Islam's behavioral requirements are interlocking—five daily prayers, dietary prohibitions, Ramadan, dress codes—you cannot dismantle just one piece and keep the rest. Once compliance breaks down, the entire behavioral structure cannot hold. At the individual level it is a switch: from complying to not complying, a clean break. But different individuals flip at different points in time; aggregated to the population level, this appears as a sloping line. A pile of switches flipping at different moments is statistically gradual; mechanistically, it is digital. The evolutionary path's gradual change is different—an individual can half-believe, go to church on Sundays but not pray during the week, call themselves Christian but not read the Bible. Evolutionary dilution occurs within the individual; lockdown rupture occurs between individuals.

**The current state of the tug-of-war.** Turkey is simultaneously experiencing external pressure and internal corrosion: urbanization continues to drain R in the background (the ratchet never stops); AKP's politicization of religion contaminates the signal end (the signal is hijacked). The lockdown path has blocked each time—but with each round, less R in hand, the younger generation's rejection more widespread. The speed of secularization is even faster than the model predicts based on a single mechanism, because both lines are running simultaneously.

### 4.3 The Self-Organization Path: Japanese Buddhism

**Why this population.** Japanese Buddhism is the case with the most complete degradation trajectory of the self-organization path. The Meiji Restoration (1868) is the precise starting point of M's entry. From the danka system (R sufficient, temples embedded in family structure) to funeral Buddhism (R at zero, temples reduced to funeral services), to the current physical extinction of temples, every stage of degradation has clear historical records. Taiwanese Buddhism is contaminated by folk religion; Thai Buddhism is contaminated by the royal institution; neither is clean enough.

**The institutionalized form of R: the danka system.** Japanese Buddhism's R has a unique institutionalized form. During the Edo period (1600–1868), the Tokugawa shogunate mandated that every household be bound to a Buddhist temple (the danka system). Families supported temples across generations; temples provided funeral and ancestral worship services for families. This system embedded all of R's components into the binding relationship between temple and family: R.kin (kin networks bound through shared temples), R.elder (ancestral worship maintaining intergenerational connections), R.ritual (annual dharma services, Obon festival, monthly memorial services), R.space (the butsudan—household Buddhist altar). The danka system was the concretization of R—intergenerational transmission of xin was not spontaneous but institutionalized into the fixed relationship between family and temple.

**The trajectory of R consumption.** After the Meiji Restoration, the danka system lost its legal compulsory force, but inertia carried it through to the postwar period. Postwar urbanization and nuclearization of the family dismantled this structure: young people left rural areas (R.kin death) → nuclear families replaced extended families (R.elder rupture) → monthly memorial services and similar customs disappeared, especially in cities (R.ritual consumption) → household Buddhist altars no longer maintained (R.space formal disappearance).

**From commercialization to physical extinction.** After the danka system collapsed, temples lost their embeddedness in family structures; the only remaining revenue source was funeral services. This is "funeral Buddhism"—Buddhism appears only when someone dies. Funeral practices became increasingly commercialized; Buddhism's role in funerals became increasingly vague. Monks themselves reported feeling like "actors on a stage" or "pawns of the funeral industry." A new secular funeral industry began competing, further squeezing the temples' last market.

Data confirms the trend toward physical extinction: between 1989 and 2019, at least 23 million people severed their ties with Buddhist temples. Within the next 25 years, an estimated 27,000 of Japan's 77,000 temples are expected to close. Within Sōtō Zen (one of the major Zen schools, 14,000 temples), the number of temples without a resident priest increased by approximately 30% between 1985 and 2005, and roughly 35% of temples have no successor. One survey predicts that by 2040, 35% of Japanese religious corporations will have disappeared.

**Verification and extension of the model.** Japanese Buddhism perfectly verifies the self-organization path's degradation sequence: when R is sufficient, temples are embedded in family structure (the danka system = the institutionalized form of R) → M dismantles family structure → R reaches zero → temples become service providers (funeral Buddhism) → form preserved, dynamics rewritten.

But the Japanese case also reveals a stage not fully predicted in the initial model: when R consumption deepens to the level of population collapse, the self-organization path moves beyond "marketization in monastic robes" to physical extinction. Temples close not because Buddhism "lost," but because there are no consumers left. Japan's declining birth rate has pushed R's floor to an extreme—not merely family redundancy at zero, but the family itself is disappearing. This exceeds the model's standard assumptions (the model assumes population exists), but can serve as a real-time case of the spiral's endpoint: M does not only consume R; M ultimately consumes the population itself.

### 4.4 Catholicism's Geographic Fracture

Model's prediction: Catholicism thrives where R is sufficient; it dies where R is exhausted.

Observed: at the start of the twentieth century, Europe held 65% of the world's Catholics; now it has dropped to approximately 20%. Catholic numbers in Africa continue to grow explosively. Where Catholicism thrives—South America, the Philippines, Africa—the common factor is that R is still sufficient and the mother is still within the family transmission structure. European Catholicism died not because Europeans stopped believing in God, but because R reached zero.

## V. Boundaries

**Issues this paper does not address:**

One. The internal mechanisms of the modernization positive feedback loop. That is the jurisdiction of *East Asian Involution Dynamics*. This paper only receives M's effects; it does not explain why M operates as it does.

Two. The truth or falsehood of doctrines. Inheriting the original paper's position—the framework does not judge whether God exists or whether enlightenment is real.

Three. Value judgments. R.mother → 0 is the value of a variable, not a judgment of good or bad. Whether the lockdown path's institutional means of protecting R are legitimate is outside the jurisdiction of dynamics.

Four. Policy recommendations. "Rebuild R" is not an operation that can be executed by policy. The recovery of R has historically occurred only spontaneously after M's collapse; there is no case of "actively restoring R while M is still running."

Five. Temporal predictions. How long each round of the spiral lasts cannot be derived from the framework.

Six. Populations beyond those selected as cases. This paper selects three of the purest cases to test mechanisms; it does not claim the model can be directly applied to all populations. South American Catholicism (semantic end half-internalized), Indonesian Muslims (impure lockdown), Taiwanese Buddhism (mixed with folk religion)—these cases are test objects for the model's boundaries, not within this paper's scope.

**Structural limitations of the framework:**

The core mechanism of this paper—R consumption, rupture of xin transmission—occurs at the semantic end. The semantic end is non-quantifiable; this is not a defect of this framework but a structural ceiling of human cognition. R's components can have proxy indicators established (female labor force participation rate, nuclear family ratio, religious ritual attendance rate), but proxy indicators quantify signal-end performance, not semantic-end transmission. "Whether the mother is at home" can be quantified; "whether the mother transmits xin" cannot.

**Challenge conditions:**

One. If someone can completely explain the different responses of the three paths under modernization pressure using a theory that does not involve R—for example, purely cognitive change, institutional inertia, or economic rationality—and the explanatory power equals or exceeds this paper's, then this paper's core model is weakened.

Two. If the evolutionary path produces a new variant that does not degrade toward the market—maintaining exclusionary elimination and zero-sum games in a low-R environment—then the prediction of evolutionary degradation is falsified.

Three. If the lockdown path successfully coexists with modernization—the signal end unchanged but R allowed to be consumed, and intergenerational transmission does not break—then the lockdown path's zero-sum structure prediction is falsified. More specifically, if after the lockdown path's institutional power is politically hijacked, the next generation does not actively reject but instead produces new adaptive variants, then the prediction of internal corrosion is falsified.

Four. If the self-organization path maintains the universality of practice rather than its stratification in a zero-R environment—that is, ordinary believers can still engage in substantive practice rather than merely consuming services under zero-redundancy conditions—then the prediction of the self-organization path's commercialization is falsified.

Five. Challenge to the underlying framework. This paper inherits all challenge conditions of Semantic Flow Dynamics and *Religion as an Information Flow System*. If the underlying framework is overturned, the upper-layer analysis simultaneously fails.

Six. If a society achieves the provision of conditions for intergenerational transmission of xin under zero-R conditions through an entirely new mechanism—for example, a digital immersion environment, AI-driven semantic transmission, virtual communities—and the effect equals that of the R provided by family and church, then R's status as the core variable is undermined. No such case currently exists, but technological development may produce one in the future.

## VI. Relationship to Existing Literature

### 6.1 Inglehart & Norris: Existential Security Theory

Inglehart attributes secularization to the increase in existential security—modern technology extends lifespan, reduces infant mortality, weakens death pressure, and reduces religious demand.

This paper's model is complementary to Inglehart's but operates one layer deeper. Inglehart describes the weakening of death pressure; this paper describes the collapse of xin's transmission mechanism. The two are mechanisms at different levels that can operate simultaneously: the weakening of death pressure reduces F's driving force; R consumption cuts off F's transmission channel. But R consumption is more fundamental than the weakening of death pressure—even if death pressure rises again (war, pandemic), if R has already reached zero, F cannot restart because the transmission channel no longer exists. Each Great Awakening occurs when death pressure rises, but cannot survive two generations, precisely because it only restarts the driving force without rebuilding the transmission channel.

### 6.2 Roof / Hervieu-Léger / Heelas & Woodhead

Roof (1999) observed the spiritual marketplace. Hervieu-Léger observed the cultural amnesia of intergenerational transmission. Heelas & Woodhead (2005) observed the shift of spirituality toward new forms.

These three observations each touched one fragment of this paper's model. This paper's contribution is not in discovering fragments but in using path differentiation to string the fragments into a causal chain: R consumption → intergenerational transmission breaks (what Hervieu-Léger saw) → the evolutionary path degrades to the market (what Roof saw) → demand flows to the self-organization path's native species (what Heelas & Woodhead saw) → the demand side was cultivated by the evolutionary path itself (what no one saw).

### 6.3 Interface with *East Asian Involution Dynamics*

*East Asian Involution Dynamics* treated T as a black box and demonstrated that the presence or absence of T determines whether a society is crushed by M. This paper treats M as a black box and analyzes how M dismantles F.

The two papers can be concatenated: M consumes R → R consumption dismantles F → after F breaks, T no longer produces social effects → society loses its interruption mechanism for M → M runs without resistance → involution. Secularization (T failing) is not an independent event; it is a downstream product of M consuming R and dismantling F.

## VII. Conclusion: The Dynamics of Civilizational Death

On the surface, this paper tracks the degradation of religion. Three paths, three cases, three response patterns. But tracked to the end, what is being tracked is not religion—it is R.

R is not religion's exclusive fuel. R is the soil of civilization. The mother's time, the family's redundancy, intergenerational co-presence—this channel carries not only the transmission of xin but all non-quantifiable intergenerational transmission: values, aesthetics, attitudes toward death, the sense of obligation to others, the capacity for long-term thinking. When R reaches zero, the channel breaks, and everything on the channel dies simultaneously. The death of religion is visible—attendance rates, identification ratios, church closures—so this paper tracks it. But the other things on the same channel die too; you cannot measure them; you can barely discuss them.

The three papers are therefore three perspectives on the same story. *Religion as an Information Flow System* identified F—the three paths of the religious positive feedback loop, the mechanism by which T is amplified into civilizational structure. *East Asian Involution Dynamics* defined T and demonstrated that the presence or absence of T determines whether a society is crushed by M. This paper tracked how M dismantles F by consuming R, thereby causing T to fail.

Put together, it is a single sentence:

```
T builds civilization → Civilization produces M → M consumes R
→ R consumption dismantles F → T fails
→ Civilization loses its interruption mechanism for M
→ M runs without resistance → Involution → M itself collapses
→ R re-accumulates → New F grows → New T → New civilization
```

(The arrows denote the sequence of dominance transfer, not linear causation. See §3.5, "The limitation of causal language.")

This is not the story of religion. This is the life cycle of civilization.

R is the soil; T and M are both plants growing from the soil. T grows first, establishing civilization's framework—community, meaning, family structure, a plan for facing death. M grows from within civilization's framework, leveraging the social infrastructure T built to begin self-reinforcing. Two plants competing for the same soil. M's returns are quantifiable; T's returns are non-quantifiable; M structurally crushes T in the competition. The soil is exhausted; both die.

The spiral is irreversible, because M permanently raises the baseline of material expectations. No one voluntarily goes back. R's recovery occurs only after M collapses—not a choice, but no choice.

We are inside the spiral right now. The three cases are not historical narratives; they are happening. The degradation of white American Protestants is complete: the label still hangs; religiosity is gone. Turkey's lockdown path is cracking from within: Generation Z is making a clean break. Japanese Buddhism's temples are physically vanishing: there are not even consumers left.

There is no exit that does not involve collapse. Not because of pessimism, but because the ratchet effect does not allow R to recover while M is running, and M will not stop on its own—a positive feedback loop has no built-in brake.

The only thing that can be stated with certainty: after collapse, R will re-accumulate, and a new F will grow. It will borrow old signal containers—scripture is still there, buildings are still there, remnant believers are still there—but new F is not old F. The signal end is recognizable; the dynamics are unrecognizable. Just as medieval Catholicism and Rome's state religion read the same Bible, but one was an appendage of M and the other was an independent force with T ≠ M. What grows in the next round will still answer the same question: humans die—then what?

**Prediction.** The reasoning above rests on a premise: after M collapses, a window exists for R to re-accumulate and for a new F to grow. Historically, this window was measured in centuries, because knowledge and technology suffered massive attrition during collapse—Rome's concrete formula was lost for a thousand years. But modern knowledge storage no longer depends on the continuous operation of human social structures. Papyrus burns; libraries need librarians; but a hard drive buried underground can still be read a hundred years later. Knowledge storage bridging collapse compresses M's restart from centuries to a decade or so—but the rebuilding of infrastructure and human capital still takes time (factories are destroyed in war; training talent takes time). R's rebuilding after collapse is forced by survival pressure; F's restart can borrow old signal containers (scripture, remnant institutions) and need not start from zero. The ultimate outcome is a race between R accumulation and M restart—if the window is long enough, a new F grows; if the window is too short, M re-absorbs everything before R accumulates, and the spiral loses its recovery phase. The outcome of this race cannot be derived from the framework.
